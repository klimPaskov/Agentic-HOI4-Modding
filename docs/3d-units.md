# 3D units with Tripo and Blender

This page is for modders who want their own 3D units on the HOI4 map: soldiers, archers, riders, beasts and vehicles. Tripo makes the model from your picture, rigs it and animates it. Blender turns the result into HOI4 files. Your coding agent does the work through the `hoi4-3d-model-pipeline` skill. You approve the picture and the credit budget, and you look at the result. New to 3D? Start with the one-page [quick start](3d-quick-start.md). The same route runs on [Tripo's Agentic Canvas](3d-canvas.md) too.

Tripo is the default generator since November 2026. Models made earlier with Meshy stay usable, and the Meshy steps still run for old jobs (see [Old Meshy jobs](#old-meshy-jobs)).

**What is proven.** Six asset types have each gone the whole route below with at least one model made from its full, detailed reference, and passed two fresh reviews in November 2026: armed humanoids (an orc with a poleaxe), a large creature (an ogre with a club), a mount (a war elephant), a winged humanoid (a bat-winged demon whose death burns him to ash), a plane and a ship. A dwarf with a glaive and a shield passed both reviews too, but its picture had lost a shoulder detail of the art, so it counts only after a run from the full picture. A review judges each clip's motion at the size of the game's designer preview and in close-up, and the still model at map size beside vanilla (D-140). **Not proven yet**: winged beasts (a four-legged dragon has not passed review), tanks, buildings and landmarks (their earlier passes were on simplified redraws of the subject and do not count), and an unarmed zombie soldier, which still reads as an ordinary soldier at map size. Do not plan a mod on those yet. See [Armed units](#armed-units) and [Known limits](#known-limits) before you spend credits.

## How to use it

1. In HOI4 Mod Setup, tick **3D models workflow** (see [Setup](#setup)). Sign in to [Tripo Studio](https://studio.tripo3d.ai) in your browser.
2. Tell your coding agent: "Make a 3D unit for `<unit>` with the `hoi4-3d-model-pipeline` skill." That skill is the one entry point. It picks the [preset](#presets) for your kind of model, which holds every Tripo setting, so you never set one by hand.
3. Approve the reference picture and the credit budget when it asks.
4. Pick how Tripo is driven:
   - **By hand.** The agent gives you one short card per model. You click through Tripo and save the export into the inbox folder. About ten minutes a model.
   - **By agent.** The agent drives Tripo in your Chrome through Claude in Chrome, while you stay signed in.
5. The agent picks the export up, picks the clips by measurement, runs the Blender stage and the checks, and shows you renders. Every step prints a one-line verdict: `pass` names the next command, `fail` names the free fix, `escalate` means no script can fix it.
6. A reviewer that did not make the unit looks at the renders. The unit is finished when it passes. Then look at it in game. If a step fails, see [When something fails](#when-something-fails).

## Presets

Each kind of model has a preset in `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/tripo_presets.json`. It holds that kind's Tripo steps and settings, the picture's pose, the free clip candidates for each role, the paid clip it may need, the checks, the credit cap and its Agentic Canvas workflow. It points at the kit's other files for the rest: the unit type in `config/unit_types.json` (its body, rig and Blender clips) or the static kind in `config/static_kinds.json` (its size, triangle budget and clips). `py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/new_job.py --list` prints them with their credits and whether the route is proven for them.

| Preset | For | Route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `humanoid` | soldier-sized bodies on two legs with empty hands | Tripo rig and clips |
| `armed_humanoid` | soldier-sized bodies on two legs with one weapon, with or without a shield | Tripo rig and clips, the weapon its own model |
| `large_creature` | two-legged bodies much bigger than a soldier, armed or not | Tripo rig and clips |
| `winged_humanoid` | two-legged bodies with wings, armed or not | Tripo rig and clips, wing chains by the kit |
| `winged_quadruped` | four-legged bodies with wings | a plain model the kit rigs |
| `mount_with_rider` | four-legged mounts that carry a rider | a plain model the kit rigs, the rider its own job |
| `quadruped` | four-legged bodies without wings or a rider | a plain model the kit rigs |
| `rider` | the person on a mount | Tripo rig and clips, seated by the kit |
| `tank` | tanks and armoured cars | static, Smart Mesh |
| `plane` | aircraft | static, Smart Mesh |
| `ship` | ships | static, Smart Mesh |
| `building` | buildings on the map | static, Smart Mesh |
| `landmark` | landmarks | static, Smart Mesh |

**Which preset.** `tripo/pick_preset.py` works it out from a few plain answers about the picture, by fixed rules, and prints one sentence to confirm with the cost of the card and of the Canvas workflow, then the `new_job.py` command to run after a yes. It refuses when no reference is given (a picture is made from references, never from a prompt alone), and a reference given with `--private` (an owner's drawing) is recorded in the job as never uploaded: it never goes to Tripo or any other online service, and the 3D-ready picture is made from it offline or by the mod's own image lane.

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/pick_preset.py --kind unit --legs 2 --weapon --hand LeftHand --name "A fighter with an axe" --slug axe_fighter --reference "the mod's art: gfx/units/axe_fighter.png"
```

Start a job from a preset, then write its card:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/new_job.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> --preset armed_humanoid orc_axeman --source "the owner's drawing: refs/drawings/orc.jpg" --set posture=upright --set weapon.name="bearded axe" --set weapon.hand=LeftHand --set weapon.grip_options.length_ratio=0.55 --set weapon.grip_options.grip=0.2
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/make_card.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> orc_axeman
```

`new_job.py` writes the whole entry of [step 2](#2-job-entry-picture-card-and-inbox) from the preset: the unit type and its anatomy, the size reference of the type's profile, the cap as the credit budget and the `tripo` block, and for a static model the job file `static_model.py` reads. It then lists what only you can answer (the sources, the posture, the weapon's name, hand and grip), and you give each with `--set` on the same command. `make_card.py` keeps only the steps of the preset's route: a static model's card has no rig or clip steps, a winged quadruped's ends at the plain model's export.

### Every unit type has its own animations

Different peoples and creatures move differently, so every distinct unit type of a mod has its own clips (D-136). A clip, a free preset or a made one, belongs to one unit type of the mod. Only a reskin or a variant of the same people shares a set: its job sets `reskin_of` to the unit it reskins. The mod keeps one record of who holds which clip, `.hoi4_3d/anim_claims.json` in the mod's root. It is a development file like `.tools/` and `.claude/`: the game ignores it, and it never ships with the mod, so leave `.hoi4_3d/` out of a Workshop upload. To keep it outside the mod, set the job's `anim_registry` to a `.json` path (relative to the jobs file) or pass `--registry <file.json>`.

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/anim_claims.py check --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> <unit>
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/anim_claims.py claim --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> <unit> --source "free preset"
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/anim_claims.py release --registry <file.json> <unit> <clip>
```

Claim a unit's clips as soon as you pick them in step 7, before the Blender stage. A claim is written under a lock and the earliest claim holds a clip, so two workers never both get one. `make_card.py` strikes out each free preset another unit of the mod holds and gives the unit's own made-clip prompt for that state, and `make_card.py` and `tripo_intake.py` refuse a job whose clips another unit holds (UNIT-ANIM-SHARED, the same rule `check_unit_files.py --mod-root` applies to the finished files). A unit whose free clips are all taken makes its own clip for each state, 20 credits each: that is the top of each preset's credit range. Trying a preset in Tripo is free and allowed, whatever the claims say. Only using it for a unit's state is refused. When a claimed clip fails a check later (the intake refuses its hip travel, say) or the unit takes another, give it back with `release` so another unit can use it. `release` takes the clip as `list` prints it, and refuses a clip another unit holds or one the unit never claimed.

## What it is

| Step | Where | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A 3D-ready picture from your art (neutral pose, empty hands) | Tripo's free image tool | you, or the agent in Chrome |
| Picture to textured model | Tripo web app | you, or the agent in Chrome |
| Rig, apply candidate animations, export | Tripo web app | you, or the agent in Chrome |
| Pick one clip per role by measurement | your PC | the agent |
| Intake, fit to HOI4, export `.mesh`, `.anim` and textures | Blender, on your PC | the agent |
| Checks, repairs, independent review | your PC | the agent and a fresh reviewer |

Tripo has no key to set up and the kit calls no Tripo API. Everything in Tripo happens in its web app, under your own sign-in, and models leave Tripo only through its own Export button. Blender is the one shared stage for every generator, so a model from Tripo, from Meshy or from your own files ends in the same checks and the same review.

### Rigging and animation

Units are rigged with Tripo's auto-rig and animated with its free preset library. The agent applies several candidate presets for each role and keeps the one that measures best (see [Pick the clips by measurement](#pick-the-clips-by-measurement)). Where no preset reads as the role, it makes one text-to-motion clip from a written instruction. Clips you made before stay in your Tripo account and can be applied to a new model for free. Manual rigging or keyframing in Blender is only for small fixes, or when no tool or preset works, and the agent records the reason in the job.

## Setup

The Blender stage is Windows only today. In HOI4 Mod Setup, tick **3D models workflow**. It installs:

- The kit's 3D pipeline (`.tools/3d_pipeline/`) and the `hoi4-3d-model-pipeline` skill.
- Blender 5.2.3, when no Blender 5.1 or 5.2 is installed. A portable Blender in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs` is found too. Set `BLENDER_EXE` to use a particular one.
- The `io_pdx_mesh` add-on for that Blender, which reads and writes HOI4's `.mesh` and `.anim` files.
- The Blender Lab MCP server, registered for your coding client. It also installs the pinned Meshy MCP server, which only old Meshy jobs use. HOI4 Mod Setup may still ask for a Meshy key. Leave it empty when you use Tripo.

**The Tripo account.** A free account can try the route (about 13 models a month, public models, non-commercial use). A paid plan gives private models, commercial use and enough credits for a unit roster. You sign in yourself. The agent never types your password.

**A new worktree or a pipeline update needs the adapter set up again.** The Blender adapter checks its own source files against hashes in `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/blender_hoi4_adapter.json`, which is not tracked. A fresh worktree has no such file, and a merge that adds or changes an adapter source makes the adapter refuse with "source closure changed ... rebootstrap" or "source checksum changed". Set it up again right after such a merge, before any queued run in that worktree reaches `run_pilot`, or the run fails there after its earlier stages. Run `py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/bootstrap_3d_workflow.py --without-meshy` from the repository. It also rewrites `.codex/config.toml` and `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/dependencies.lock.json` with paths of your machine. Put those two back with `git checkout`, and never commit them. It leaves a background Blender running as an MCP server (`--command blender_mcp`). Stop that one process before a run that waits for no Blender to be running.

The setup prints a JSON record, and two lines in it are expected on a machine without Claude Code's command line (`claude`). `"claude_registration": "claude_cli_not_found"` means the `claude` command is not on the PATH, so the Meshy and Blender routes were not added to Claude Code's local settings. The Codex routes are written, and the Tripo route needs no registration, because the scripts start Blender themselves. `"template_removed": true` means the temporary `.codex/3d_mcp_config.template.toml` was deleted after the real MCP config was written, and it is true on every run. The setup also prints both in words. The setup waits for the machine like every Blender start (see [step 8](#8-intake-free)).

**By agent needs Chrome.** The agent uploads your pictures and collects the exports through the Claude in Chrome extension. In Chrome's settings, turn off "Ask where to save each file before downloading", so exports land in your Downloads folder without a dialog. A browser pane inside a coding client cannot upload a local picture, and it asks you to confirm each download, so it only suits the by-hand mode.

## The route step by step

Run every command from the repository root. Your agent writes the job registry (`3d_jobs.json`) with one entry per unit: its `unit_type`, `"provider": "tripo"`, `credit_budget`, `anatomy`, its size, the picture's details and a `tripo` block with the clip for each role. [Step 2](#2-job-entry-picture-card-and-inbox) has a complete entry to copy. `<job folder>` in the commands below is `docs/assets/hoi4_3d_model_jobs/models_3d/<unit>`, the folder the scripts make for the unit.

**Size.** `target_height_m` is the unit's mesh height in HOI4 units, not metres, and `runtime_entity_scale` is its entity scale. Vanilla infantry is 7.35 tall at scale 0.8. Give a soldier-sized unit 7.35, and scale that for a smaller or larger body. `run_pilot.py` refuses a job whose unit would stand under half or over three times the height of its vanilla scale reference on the map. Review sheets frame every unit to fit, so they never show a wrong size.

### 1. A 3D-ready picture

Make or fix this picture in Tripo's own Image tool (in the Tripo web app, under the user's sign-in). That is the one route for it. It needs no `hoi4-imagegen` server, which may not be connected at all, and the `hoi4-imagegen` skill has no route for it.

**The picture decides the look.** A model's colours come from its picture and the generator's texture. They are never changed afterwards by a tint, a filter or a recolour over that texture, for any unit. When the look must change (a unit that does not read as what it is at map size), make a new picture and generate the model again. The Blender stage refuses a Tripo unit whose diffuse map differs from the generator's export by a global colour transform: one 3 by 3 colour matrix and offset that explains the change with a residual of at most 35% of it, for a change of at least 3 of 255 RMS (`lib/texture_look.py`, `diffuse_look` in the texture report).

**The picture comes from references, never from nothing.** A text prompt alone never makes a reference picture. Follow these steps for every unit and static model:

1. Start from the subject's own reference: the owner's drawing, or the mod's existing art for it.
2. When the subject is a real thing (a tank, a ship, a uniform, a building style), research it first. Find real references: photographs, plans, museum pieces, period art. Save each in the job's `refs` folder, or note its link or book and page.
3. Give the subject's reference and the researched references to the Image tool together, and name in the instruction what each one shows.
4. List every reference in the job's `reference_sources` (one entry each: a repository path, a link, or a book and page) and in the card's Sources row, before the first paid press. The picture review (`init_pilot_jobs.py --record-visual-review`) refuses a job whose list is empty, and a picture without its sources is not used.

**A picture for a building or a landmark.** The rules for poses and hands below are for units. A static model's picture follows these instead:

- One building, elevated three-quarter view, the whole of it in frame, plain light grey background, soft even light with no cast shadows.
- **No plate base for a map building.** Vanilla's map buildings stand on the terrain with at most a small yard round them. A big square paved plate sits on the map like a tile. Ask for only a small yard just round the building, with a ragged edge that can sink into the terrain.
- **Thick bracing.** Thin open members (trestle posts, cross-braces, crane beams, railings, lattice) break in generation into open sheets and torn faces. Ask for every post, brace and beam to be thick and chunky, at least about 1.5 % of the building's longest side (a 0.3 m post on a 20 m hall). Before you pay, look at the picture: redraw it, free, when a brace still looks thinner than that.
- No smoke, falling water, people or trees. The map draws its own effects and scenery.
- Keep the mod's own look: when the mod wants a hand-sculpted, hand-painted maquette, ask for that and not for "photographic".

Tripo copies the picture's pose into the rig, so the picture must be ready for rigging:

- One subject, full body, front view, feet visible, on a plain light background.
- Arms hang relaxed about 30 degrees away from the body, with background showing between each arm and the torso.
- Both hands empty, open and relaxed. A held weapon or a clenched fist rigs badly and deforms.
- Every detail of your art kept: face, clothing, gear on the correct side.

If your art is not like this, the agent redraws it in Tripo's image tool (**Image**, model Nano Banana 2, free within the quota) with an instruction that names every detail to keep and only the pose and hands to change. The tool's page is `studio.tripo3d.ai/workspace/generate-image`. In Tripo Studio it is the **Image** icon at the top of the left sidebar. Name gear by the side of the picture ("on the viewer's left"), because "his left" gets swapped. Gear the art shows on one side only is named "only on the viewer's left". Where the art hides a side (a wrist behind a weapon, an arm behind the body), write that it is not visible and must not be added ("the viewer's right wrist is not visible, do not add a bracer there"), or the redraw copies the gear to both sides. Check the result against the original before you pay for a model. You approve the picture and answer the anatomy questions in `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/body_presets.json`.

**A look change is also a redraw.** When the unit's look must change (another colour, another uniform, damage), make a new 3D-ready picture in the same tool: the instruction names the new look and every other detail kept, and a new model comes from it. Never apply a tint or filter to the old picture or to a model's texture.

Three rules hold for every unit and asset, at every step:

- **Never simplify the subject.** A model is made as designed and as drawn, with all its detail. A 3D-ready picture may change the pose and leave out what is not body or gear, nothing else. When a source mesh has defects, change the method (another Tripo mode or setting, more triangles, a repair step, separate parts) and keep the design. The same holds for pictures, clip choices, text and requirements: nothing is cut down to make a step easier or a check pass without the owner's consent. A result made from a simplified input is never reported as passed, and every reported pass names the reference it used.
- **Never change a look with a filter.** A model's colours or look are never changed by a tint, filter or recolour over its existing texture. When the look must change, make a new reference picture and a new model from it.
- **When the detailed version cannot be done,** stop and ask the owner, showing the detailed target and what would be lost.

What each field is for:

| Field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| the key (`undead_soldier`) | The name you type on the command line. It equals the part of `asset_id` after the last dot, and the job folder is named after it |
| `asset_id` | `hoi4_3d_model_jobs.` and the key |
| `unit_type` | A type from `config/unit_types.json` (see [Unit types and bodies](#unit-types-and-bodies)). It sets the profile, the six roles and the rig |
| `provider` | `"tripo"`. Without it the job runs the old Meshy route |
| `runtime_stem` | The file name stem of the finished `.mesh` and `.anim` files |
| `credit_budget` | The credits you approved. The scripts only print it, and the agent stops spending at it |
| `anatomy` | Answers to the questions in `config/body_presets.json`, from the picture. `init_pilot_jobs.py` copies them and `unit_type` into the job, and `measure_body.py` checks them against the model. These seven keys are the only ones it reads, and a weapon is not one of them |
| `reference_path` | The picture, relative to the job folder. It must be a real PNG in `refs/original`, the only image there |
| `reference_sources` | Every reference the picture was made from, one entry each: the subject's own reference (the owner's drawing, the mod's art), and for a real-world subject the real references research found (a repository path, a link, or a book and page). It goes into the provenance record, and the picture review refuses a job whose list is empty (see step 1) |
| `reference_source_mode`, `reference_generator_output` | Where the picture came from. They go into the job's provenance record |
| `asset_brief`, `required_components`, `forbidden_additions` | What the unit is, what must be in it and what must not. They go into the job's brief and the reviewer's checklist |
| `proposed_runtime_identifiers` | `{}` until the unit is wired into a mod |
| `target_height_m`, `runtime_entity_scale` | The size (see Size above) |
| `vanilla_scale_reference` | The vanilla soldier the size is judged against. Copy it as it is. The `mesh` is a file of the installed game, found by the kit's game folder rule |
| `tripo.roles` | One clip name for each of the six roles, read from the export (step 7). `tripo.trim` and the other optional `tripo` keys are in `tripo_intake.py` |
| `tripo.posture` | `upright` or `hunched`, the idle's outline on the map camera (step 7) |
| `tripo.weapon` | Only for an armed unit: the weapon's mesh, its bone and where it comes from (see [Armed units](#armed-units)). An unarmed unit has none. The rest of `tripo` is explained in [Tripo defaults](#tripo-defaults) |
| `tripo.weapon.hand` | The hand that holds the weapon, `LeftHand` or `RightHand`, named by the rig's side. The picture is a front view, so a weapon on the viewer's right is in the `LeftHand` |
| `tripo.weapon.name` | The weapon as the art shows it ("wooden mallet"). The card's weapon steps use it |
| `tripo.weapon.grip_options` | The grip stage's numbers. `length_ratio` is the weapon's length over the body's height and `grip` is where the hand holds it, as a share of the length from the butt, both read from the art. `hold_hand`, `roll` and `head_toward` are optional (`tripo/grip_weapon.py`) |

**The job folder and the picture.** Make the folder `docs/assets/hoi4_3d_model_jobs/models_3d/<unit>/refs/original` and put the approved picture in it, as a real PNG named as in `reference_path` (Tripo's image tool saves WebP under a `.png` name, so convert it first). It is the only file there.

**Tripo jobs use `init_pilot_jobs.py` only for the picture.** It needs no Meshy key and makes no provider call. It records the picture in the job, and `run_pilot.py` (step 9) refuses a unit whose picture has not been reviewed. Run it, look at the picture yourself, then record your review:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/init_pilot_jobs.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> <unit>
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/init_pilot_jobs.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> --record-visual-review <unit> --reviewer <your name> --review-file <answers.json>
```

`<answers.json>` is a file inside the repository that answers true or false for each check, with an optional note:

```json
{
  "single_subject": true,
  "complete_silhouette": true,
  "component_separation": true,
  "neutral_background": true,
  "no_multi_view_board": true,
  "no_side_profile_sheet": true,
  "notes": "One subject, full body, front view, plain light background, arms away from the body, hands empty."
}
```

If the entry lacks a field the script needs, it stops and names the missing fields. The review reads `reference_sources` from the entry as it is when you record the review, so sources added after the first run count without initializing the job again. The intake (step 8) does not need this step to run, but `run_pilot.py` does.

**The card and the inbox.** Write the card with the kit, not by hand:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/make_card.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> <unit>
```

It fills `.tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/CARD_TEMPLATE.md` from the job entry and its preset into `<job folder>/CARD.md`: the preset, the picture, the Sources row, every Tripo setting, the candidate presets for each role, the file name to save under and the kit commit it was written from. An armed unit's card also has the weapon picture, the weapon model and the grip step, with the hand and the file names filled in. It refuses, and lists what to add, an entry that would leave a step open: no `reference_sources`, an armed unit type without `tripo.weapon`, a weapon without `hand`, `name` or `grip_options`, or a unit without `tripo.posture`. If you pull a newer kit during a run, write the card again. In the by-hand mode you follow it. In the by-agent mode the agent follows it.

### 3. Model (Tripo, 30 credits)

In Tripo Studio open **Model**, **HD Model**, add the picture, reset every setting to the card and check that **Generate** shows 30 (see [Tripo settings](#tripo-settings)), then generate. One good picture is enough. Look at the result from all sides before you pay for the rig.

**Check the span before the rig.** On the model's page press **Export** (GLB, free) and run:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/span_check.py <model.glb> <job folder>/refs/original/<picture>.png
```

It compares the model's width over its height with the picture subject's. TRIPO-SPAN-PICTURE fails when they differ by more than a tenth: wings, arms or a tail came out shorter or longer than drawn, and no later step brings the span back. Make the model again before you pay for the rig. The bat-winged demon's picture and model both read 1.51.

**Check a quadruped's neck and legs before the rig.** For a mount, a large beast or a winged quadruped, read two shares off the picture in pixels: the neck from where it leaves the shoulders to the jaw's root, and the belly's height between the legs, each over the subject's height from the feet to the top of the head (horns included, wings left out). Then run:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/proportion_check.py <model.glb> --neck <share> --legs <share>
```

Check the 3D-ready picture and any multi-view the same way before the paid press: read the shares off the redrawn picture, or a multi-view's side view, and run `proportion_check.py --shares neck=<share>,legs=<share> --neck <share> --legs <share>` against the owner's reference. The Tripo dragon's multi-view had already lost the long neck its model then lacked. On the model, it measures the neck from where it leaves the chest, seen from the side, to the head's root, so a neck that rises from the breast through a ruff of shoulder spikes counts in full. TRIPO-PROPORTION-PICTURE fails when a share differs from the picture's by more than three tenths of it: make the model again from a picture that shows the part plainly, not the rig. The Tripo dragon's picture has a long S-shaped neck (0.22 of its height) and its first model a short column (0.12). The model remade from a redraw that shows the neck plainly keeps it (0.23). Four rig rounds were spent on poses that read as a hunch before a fresh review named the short neck.

### 4. Rig (Tripo, 20 credits)

Open **Rig**: **Humanoid**, skeleton preset **Mixamo**, **Auto Rig**.

### 5. Candidate animations (Tripo, free)

Open **Animate**. Its page is `studio.tripo3d.ai/workspace/animate/<asset id>`. The asset id is in the address of your model's page, `studio.tripo3d.ai/workspace/generate/<asset id>`: open the model from the history list on the left and copy what follows the last `/`. Use the address, because the sidebar's icons change order from page to page and some open paid tools. Apply every candidate preset on the card, using the search box to find each one by name. A good set for a humanoid is `idle`, `walk`, `hit_to_stomach`, `hit_to_body_01`, `flee_01`, `defeat`, `defeat_02` and `fall`. Every clip must fit the unit, its era, its weapon and its kind of creature: the boxing, kick and dance presets never do, even when they pass the checks (D-127, `never_fitting` in `config/tripo_presets.json`), and `make_card.py` and the intake refuse a job that names one. Clips you made earlier are under **AI Animation** and cost nothing to apply again.

If no candidate can read as the attack, write one text-to-motion clip (20 credits). Name the motion, the hand, the height and the stance, for example "lunges one step forward, both arms straight at shoulder height, grabbing, torso upright, feet planted". The clip is named after the instruction text.

### 6. Export into the inbox

On the Animate page press **Export**: format **GLB**, **Export Skeleton** on, **Animation stay in Place** on. The dialog remembers each switch from the last export, so read a switch before you touch it (`aria-checked` is `true` when it is on) and click it only when it reads off. `TS.setStayInPlace(true)` in `tripo/tripo_studio.js` does this for an agent, and `TS.startExport()` refuses while the switch reads off. Under **Number of Animations** press **Select All**. Tripo builds the file in about a minute and saves it as `tripo_convert_<id>.glb`. Move it into the job's inbox as `<asset>__rigged.glb`. An armed unit's body goes to `<job folder>/armed/<asset>__body.glb` instead, and the grip stage writes the inbox file (see [Armed units](#armed-units)).

A Model page export is named after the model's title (`orc character 3d model.glb`), so rename it as the card says. A new asset on the Generate page keeps the last asset's settings but not its picture: add the picture again and read the settings before you press.

**What the body is called.** A one-piece model (step 3 with **Generate in Parts** off) exports as one skinned mesh. Its mesh node is named `tripo_node_<id>` and its mesh `tripo_mesh_<id>`, not `tripo_part_0`. No Segment step is needed for it. Segment is only for a separately modelled weapon or shield (see [Armed units](#armed-units)). A tool that takes a body name (`clip_check.py --body`) uses the one skinned mesh when you leave the name out. An unarmed unit has no `tripo_part_1` either, so such a tool checks the body alone.

With **Animation stay in Place** off, every clip carries its travel in the hips, and the loop, grounding and weapon-drop rules fail on it. The intake and the grip stage measure each role clip's hips travel in the file (`lib/glb_root.py`) and refuse one that moves more than a tenth of the hip height.

Add clips later to the same rigged model the body came from. Tripo can hold several rigs of one model under different tasks, and a new rig does not match the old one: on the orc the joint rotations differed by up to 0.77 in quaternion distance, and the weapon settings tuned for the first rig put the axe into the body. Compare the vertex count and the joints' rest transforms with the earlier export before you use the new one.

Two preset names change on export: the tile `defeat` exports as `defeat_02`, and the tile `defeat_02` exports as `defeat_03`. Read the clip names from the exported file, not from the tiles.

### 7. Pick the clips (free)

The agent measures every clip in the export and writes the role map into the job's `tripo.roles` (see [Pick the clips by measurement](#pick-the-clips-by-measurement)). `clip_check.py` gives each clip's stretch and ground numbers. A role's value is the clip's exported name, or its first words for a text-to-motion clip.

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/clip_check.py <job folder>/inbox/<asset>__rigged.glb --samples all --json <job folder>/evidence/clips.json
```

For an unarmed unit this command is complete. It finds the body and no weapon by itself, prints `no weapon`, and measures stretch and the ground only. An armed unit's body export (`armed/<asset>__body.glb`) has no weapon yet, so the same command measures it the same way.

**Decide the attack before you leave Tripo.** A text-to-motion clip is made on the same rigged model, so measure the first export while the Tripo tab is still open. Add `--attack <candidates>` to the command above: it ends with one `attack:` line, `attack: pass <clips>` or `attack: none passes` with exit code 3. On `none passes`, make the text-to-motion attack now, export again in a fresh tab and measure again. The hobgoblin run (2026-11-22) left Tripo first and learnt only then that `chop`, its one free candidate, failed the stretch (8.9%, limit 5%).

**Each clip must read as its role and as the unit's kind.** A player watches a unit's animations in the game's designer preview, so a clip is judged there, on the review's designer preview sheet, and in Blender close-ups (D-140). The preview frame is 249 pixels square in `interface/divisiondesignerview.gui` (`toggle_model_selector_button`), and the model picker's view is 510 by 316 (`border_container` in `div_template_select_model`). `lib/designer_preview.py` reads both from the installed game. No interface or define file sets the camera inside the frame, so the sheet fits the unit to the frame as the close-up sheets do, until an in-game look confirms that share. On the map a unit is about 60 pixels tall, and there the still model must read as the unit beside vanilla. A clean clip can still fail review because it reads as something else. Before the run, `tripo/pose_check.py --map` reads each clip's silhouette against the idle at map size (TRIPO-MAP-READ) and its outline by the rules of `lib/map_outline.py` (see [Tripo checks](#tripo-checks)). Those readings are advice that helps choose between candidates, never a reason to drop one. Pick for each role:

- the attack: a strike whose outline changes clearly from the idle, not a guard held for most of the clip,
- the defend: a guard or recoil whose outline differs from the attack and from the idle,
- the idle and the move: a posture that says what the unit is. An undead soldier hunches, sways and shambles with the arms reaching forward at shoulder height. A healthy soldier stands straight. Arms raised above the head read as surrender,
- the retreat: a run that differs from the move.

When no preset reads, make the role's clip with Tripo's text-to-motion (20 credits), with a prompt that names the outline you need. A new texture or picture never fixes a clip that reads wrong: an undead soldier with a corpse's skin still reads as a living soldier at map zoom when it stands and walks like one.

**The clip choice, step by step.** Do these in order and write each number into the job before the run.

1. Write the idle posture into the job's `tripo` block: `"posture": "upright"` for a soldier, `"posture": "hunched"` for an undead, a beast-man or a cowering creature. TRIPO-MAP-POSTURE reports, as advice, whether the exported idle reads that way.
2. Apply every free candidate on the model's Animate page: the presets on the card, and every earlier clip under **AI Animation** that was made for the same kind of unit (they apply to a new model for nothing). Click the middle of a tile, not the round mark in its corner, and wait until **Retargeting** has gone before the next tile.
3. Export once (step 6) and run `tripo/pose_check.py <rigged.glb> <earlier run's .mesh> --unarmed --map --idle "<first words of the idle you plan>" --attack <attack candidates> --gait <move and retreat candidates>`. A first run has no earlier `.mesh` of the same body. Then write each role by the `clip_check.py` numbers (the candidate with the lowest stretch that passes), run the unit once to the end, and do this step again on that run's `runtime/files/<runtime_stem>.mesh`. An armed unit runs it on `armed/<asset>__body.glb` with `--hand <the job's tripo.weapon.hand>` in place of `--unarmed`.
4. Pick the idle first, because every map reading is taken against it. Read the `idle posture` line: it should name the posture the job asks for. When the job asks for `hunched` and no Tripo idle reads hunched (Tripo's "slightly hunched" idle reads 0.98, upright), keep the best idle and set `"idle_pose": "hunch"`. The route then bends it, and the hunch reads 0.88.
5. Then pick, by the tool's lines: an attack and a defend that pass `verdict`, and the move and the retreat with a loop window. Of those, prefer an attack that reads at least 300 px and a defend that reads at least 160 px at map size. These two numbers are advice (D-140): a clip under them is still picked when no candidate reads better.
6. Read the `outline` lines for the attack and the defend. A hand spread of 0.80 or more can read as a T-pose or a flail. A strike under 5 px outside the body can foreshorten into the body from the high front camera. An attack and a defend that are both wide spreads can read as each other. These are advice: the `attack pick` line picks an attack that meets them before one that does not, and drops none for them. On the D-94 zombie's export (2026-11-22) Tripo's idle read 0.98, the sudden lunge spread 0.41 and struck 11 px outside the body, the clawing swipe struck 7 px, and `box_01` struck 0 px.
7. A role with no candidate gets one text-to-motion clip. Write the ledger row first. The prompt names the stance, the limbs, the height of the hands and the speed, for example "lunges one huge fast step, both arms thrust straight forward at chest height, then jerks back". Keep the prompt at 280 characters or fewer: Tripo refuses a longer one without a word (no task, no charge, the button stays lit). An agent runs `TS.motionPromptCheck()` from `tripo/tripo_studio.js` before each press and presses only on `ok: true`. If the balance has not dropped a few seconds after the press, shorten the prompt before pressing again. After a page reload, apply one free clip first so the model is loaded, or the press does nothing. Export again in a fresh browser tab and measure it as in step 2.
8. Stop when the role's budget is spent. Record which limit each candidate missed and by how much, run the unit with the closest one, and ask before more credits.

Two things that make an attack fail the strike and the map reading, seen on the D-94 zombie (2026-11-21): **Animation stay in Place** strips a lunge's step forward, so the hips do not travel and a lunge counts only its arms (two lunging grabs measured 0.20 and 0.22 of the height at 0.8 and 1.2 a second, against 0.25 and 1.0). And when the idle already holds the arms forward, as a zombie's does, a two-handed grab changes its outline little (169 to 272 px against 300). A third clip that asked for a wind-up with the hands pulled back to the chest, then a hard two-handed thrust, came out weaker still (0.13 at 0.54 a second, 138 px): Tripo did not make the wind-up. Text-to-motion alone has not given this kind of attack.

**A lunging attack keeps its step.** When the attack is a lunge, give it back the step that **Animation stay in Place** removed:

1. Export once more with **Animation stay in Place** off (free, a fresh tab). Name the file `<asset>__travel.glb` and keep it beside the inbox, not in it.
2. Run `py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/lunge_clip.py <inbox>/<asset>__rigged.glb <asset>__travel.glb --clip "<first words of the lunge>" --out <inbox>/<asset>__rigged.glb --cap 0.6`. It copies that clip's forward step into the in-place file, capped at `--cap` hip heights, and keeps every other clip and the up-and-down motion as they were. The Blender stage retargets every clip in place, so after the export the route puts the step back on the attack from the role's provider file and eases it back to the start over the clip's last 30% (`lib/tripo_lunge.py`): run zombie_b, before this step, exported the attack with no step and it read 270 px.
3. Add `"lunge": {"attack": 0.6}` to the job's `tripo` block. The intake then allows that clip's travel up to the cap, and for an unarmed unit TRIPO-ATTACK-NO-STRIKE measures the hands against the ground instead of the chest, because the chest moves with them in a lunge. The limits stay 0.25 and 1.0. Measure before the run with `pose_check.py ... --lunge`. When the arm level's solve still leaves the hands over the head in too many frames (TRIPO-HANDS-HIGH), it lowers both arms a further 2.5 degrees at a time, up to 30, while the hands stay ahead of the chest.
4. Pick the cap as the smallest that reaches the map reading. On the D-94 zombie (2026-11-21) the sudden lunge stepped 1.23 hip heights. Capped at 0.3 it read 259 px, at 0.6 it read 587 px, and its swell fell from 0.090 to 0.065. Against the chest it struck 0.23 at 0.97 a second, and against the ground 0.47 at 1.41. The lunge with its arms held out and no step that reviewers read as no strike (zombie_o) still measures 0.13 at 0.37 against the ground, so a held pose does not pass this way. Two fresh reviews still decide whether the attack reads as a lunging grab.

### 8. Intake (free)

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo_intake.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> <unit> --inbox <job folder>/inbox --credits <spent> --credits-note "<steps>"
```

The intake records each file's name, size and SHA-256, then prepares it for the shared stage in Blender. It renames Tripo's bones to the kit's names, folds the finger bones into the hands so the unit stays within HOI4's 50 bones, writes one file per role, and applies the [Tripo defaults](#tripo-defaults).

**Extra joints.** Tripo's auto-rig may add extra joints to the Mixamo skeleton. Since November 2026 it adds `neutral_bone`: a joint under the armature with no children and no animation, that the exports of the days before did not have. The intake drops such a joint (a childless, unanimated joint outside the hips chain). It moves any vertex weight on it to the kit bone nearest to the vertex, and to the hips when no kit bone is near. It prints one line, for example `extra joints dropped: neutral_bone (1 joint, 1 vertex weight moved to the nearest kit bone)`. A joint outside the chain that has children or animation stops the intake and is named: export the rig again.

**Blender starts here.** Blender is the heavy step, and your machine may already run a game, a render or another unit's Blender. Every kit command that starts Blender first waits for the machine (`lib/heavy_job.py`):

- It takes a lock file, `hoi4-kit-heavy-job.lock` in the temporary folder, so only one kit Blender runs at a time.
- It waits while the free RAM is under 4 GB (`HOI4_KIT_MIN_FREE_RAM_GB` sets another floor).
- It prints one line saying why it waits, and gives up with an error after six hours (`HOI4_KIT_HEAVY_WAIT_MINUTES`).
- `--skip-heavy-check`, or `HOI4_KIT_SKIP_HEAVY_CHECK=1`, starts at once.

The commands that start Blender, and so wait: `bootstrap_3d_workflow.py`, `tripo_intake.py` (this step), `run_pilot.py` (step 9, through the Blender adapter), `review_unit.py`, `repair_unit.py`, `check_holes.py` and `fit_prop.py` (step 10), and `tripo/colour_compare.py`, `tripo/weapon_sheets.py`, `tripo/static_model.py`, `tripo/static_sheets.py` and `tripo/effects_sheet.py`. `tripo/arm_unit.py` waits too, before each of its grip and shield stages. A Blender command you type yourself, such as the stage scripts run with `blender -b --python` (`grip_weapon.py`, `shield_stage.py`, `hand_views.py`), waits only when you put `py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/lib/heavy_job.py --` in front of it.

**One gate per step.** The kit needs no other gate. Some machines add their own gate script around Blender (a wrapper that also waits for a game or a shared hold file). Such a script lives on that machine, outside the kit, and the kit does not ship one. When your machine has one, run each Blender step through it and nothing more. Never take that machine's hold yourself for the steps you then run through its gate: the gate waits while any hold exists, so the step would wait for your own hold until its timeout. A hold is only for heavy work that does not go through the gate.

### 9. Blender stage and export (free)

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/run_pilot.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> <unit>
```

Blender scales the unit against vanilla infantry, grounds every clip, and writes the `.mesh`, one `.anim` per role and three DXT5 textures with mip levels, then imports them again to prove they load.

### 10. Checks, repair and review (free)

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/measure_body.py <job folder>
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/check_unit_files.py <unit folder> --unit-type <type> --mod-root . --weapon-bone <tripo.weapon.bone> --verdict <job folder>/evidence/check.json
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/check_holes.py <unit folder> --out <job folder>/evidence/holes
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/tripo_checks.py <unit folder> --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> --slug <unit> --json <job folder>/evidence/tripo_checks.json
```

Leave out `--weapon-bone` for a unit with no weapon. An armed unit that lets its weapon go in the death passes the bone its job's `tripo.weapon.bone` names, usually `Weapon`, or the dropped weapon fails UNIT-LOOSE-PART as a part that drifts off the body.

`measure_body.py` checks that the model fits the job's unit type (see [Check the body before you rig it](#check-the-body-before-you-rig-it)). `check_unit_files.py` reads the exported files (see [Automatic checks](#automatic-checks)), and `check_holes.py` looks for holes a player would see (see [Holes the player sees](#holes-the-player-sees)). `tripo_checks.py` adds the checks for faults Tripo's clips make (see [Tripo checks](#tripo-checks)). Each verdict names the repair to run, or escalates.

`repair_unit.py` fixes the mesh faults the checks name, in Blender, with no credits. The verdict gives the exact command, for example:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/repair_unit.py <unit folder> --out <job folder>/repair --repairs winding holes
```

| Repair | Fixes |
| --- | --- |
| `winding` | Triangles facing the wrong way and inside-out parts (UNIT-WINDING, UNIT-INSIDE-OUT) |
| `bridges` | Surfaces fusing body parts that move apart, such as an arm pressed into the hips. The joining faces are cut and both sides closed (UNIT-BRIDGE). A bridge inside one continuous skin, such as an ear root, is not cut but listed under `weight_seams` for `weights` |
| `holes` | Open loops of at most `--max-hole-edges` edges, 16 by default. The check suggests 32 (UNIT-HOLE) |
| `weights` | Hard weight edges inside one skin, and a crotch whose two sides follow one leg each (UNIT-SEAM). Weights are blended over a few rings, then each vertex keeps at most four influences |
| `ground` | Feet off or under the ground: the root's translation keys are shifted so the lowest point meets the ground in every frame. A death clip is only lifted where it sinks (UNIT-ANIM-GROUND). Needs no Blender |

Repairs never make new bones, a new weight set or new keyframes. The copy goes to `<job folder>/repair/files`, and `repair_report.json` lists what changed with the check results before and after. Nothing under `gfx/` changes.

`review_unit.py` then renders every clip, and a reviewer who did not make the unit lists every defect it sees. The unit is finished only when it passes (see [Independent review](#independent-review)). The review judges each clip at the size of the game's designer preview and the still model at map size, so make these sheets before you ask for a review. Each starts Blender:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/weapon_sheets.py <unit folder> --out <review folder> --sheet preview
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/weapon_sheets.py <unit folder> --out <review folder> --sheet game
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/clip_sheet.py <unit folder> --roles death --frames 6 --views left --out <review folder>/sheets/death_six_frames_side.png
```

The first writes `sheets/preview_size.png`: every clip in six frames, a front three-quarter view, in tiles 249 pixels square, the size of the game's designer preview (`lib/designer_preview.py`). Reviewers judge each clip's motion there and on the close-ups (D-140). The second writes `sheets/game_size.png` (the unit about 60 pixels tall from the game's camera and from the front, four frames of every clip) and `sheets/game_size_x3.png`, the same sheet three times larger for reading. It judges the still model's size and silhouette beside vanilla, not the motion. The third writes the death in six frames from the side. A review folder without them is not ready.

A welded non-manifold edge (an edge shared by more than two faces) stops `run_pilot.py` at its candidate gate when the profile allows none. Tripo HD models made of many closed parts can bring a few. No repair for them runs before the export yet. When `check_holes.py` and `measure_body.py` pass on the provider GLB, record a reasoned exception for that one job (the measured count, the reason and the job as its scope, in the form `profile_exceptions` reads) and continue. Never widen the profile for every job. For colour, `tripo/colour_compare.py` renders the unit and a vanilla unit under the same light and prints the brightness and saturation of each.

**Effects.** A unit that fires, casts, burns or blows up gets its particles in [the effects step](3d-effects.md), after these checks and before the review: `tripo/effects_stage.py` places the locators, times the events and checks them, and `tripo/effects_sheet.py` draws them as stamped stand-ins. Each effect must fit the unit (D-127): a world without gunpowder never gets a muzzle flash.

### 11. Hand-over and in game

The export lands in the job's `runtime/files` folder under its final names, next to `runtime/registration.md`, which holds the `.gfx`, `.asset` and entity text. The newest copy that passed its check is the one you review. Put it into the mod only after a fresh reviewer passes it, with step 12. Then you, or your live-testing setup, place the unit in game and look at it idle, moving and fighting.

### 12. Wire the unit into the mod (free)

Run this only after the fresh review passes. `wire_unit.py` puts the job's registration into your mod and says what the mod still lacks:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/wire_unit.py <job folder or unit slug> --mod-root <mod> [--dry-run] [--json]
```

It writes the `pdxmesh`, animation and entity blocks, each between `# wire_unit begin <stem> <kind>` and `# wire_unit end <stem> <kind>` comments. A rerun replaces only the lines between its own markers, and nothing else in a file is touched. It copies the mesh, animations and textures into the model folder that `registration.md` names, and skips a file whose SHA-256 already matches. The `.gfx` and entity `.asset` go to `gfx/entities/<prefix>_units.gfx` and `.asset`, or to the file that already holds the markers. `--gfx-file` and `--entity-file` choose other files, and `--prefix` sets the prefix. If the mod already defines one of the names outside the markers, it stops and writes nothing.

It never writes sub-units or localisation. It checks that a sub-unit has `sprite = <stem>` and that the keys `<id>` and `<id>_desc` exist, and lists what is missing. It lists the counter sprites `GFX_unit_<sprite>_icon_medium` and `_medium_white` and whether the mod or only the game defines each. It writes each sub-unit's text icon `GFX_unit_<id>_icon_small` into `interface/<prefix>_texticons.gfx`, between its markers and with the game's art for the sub-unit's `group`, unless the mod defines it already. Without it the game logs "Couldnt find texticon" on every counter refresh. It warns when `descriptor.mod` sets no `supported_version`, which a scratch test mod needs (for example `supported_version="1.19.*"`).

Add a `sound_plan` to the job to get sound events: a list of `{role, soundeffect, frame}`. The tool writes `event = { time = <seconds> sound = { soundeffect = "..." } }` into the entity state for that role, at the frame divided by 30. An event's `time` is seconds of the clip: vanilla's destroyed landmarks fire their explosions within 0.3 s of the frame their clip hides a part ([effects](3d-effects.md#what-an-effect-is-in-hoi4)). Earlier versions wrote the frame number itself, so a sound planned at frame 12 played 12 seconds in, after the clip had ended. The effects step's sounds join the plan at their particles' frames. Sounds are never generated. Without a plan it prints a "no sound plan" warning.

After writing, it runs `check_unit_files.py --mod-root` and the static wiring audit and prints one verdict, PASS or FAIL with the list. A missing sprite or localisation key is a FAIL but does not stop the write. Only audit errors about this unit count, and `--strict-audit` counts every error in the mod. `--dry-run` prints the plan, changes nothing, and ends `DRY-RUN` or `DRY-RUN-FAIL`. The exit code is 1 on any FAIL and 2 on unreadable input.

**When Tripo cannot rig the body.** Some bodies defeat Tripo's auto-rig. The ZIN devil, with wings 2.8 times its height, got five rigs and one usable one after `tripo/arm_seat.py`, and on that one Tripo's idle and walk pulled both feet to the middle. Then move the clips from a body Tripo rigs well:

1. Keep one motion donor in your Tripo account: a plain humanoid that belongs to no unit, rigged once (20). One of Tripo's demo characters will do (the ZIN devil's donor was the demo goblin). Note its asset id, and reuse it for every later unit instead of rigging another. Apply the unit's claimed clips there (presets and made clips, each unit type its own, all free to apply) and export it. The clips stay claimed by your unit. The donor only lends the motion.
2. Run `py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/clip_transfer.py <source export> <your repaired export> --out <job folder>/inbox/<asset>__rigged.glb --clips <names>`. Each bone's turn from its rest pose is carried through the two bodies' own frames (across the thighs, up, ahead) onto your rig's rest pose, so your bones keep their lengths and the legs keep their own stance. The hips keep the source's movement, scaled by the hip heights, less the clip's mean level offset, since the route plays clips in place (`lib/clip_transfer.py`). Each body's frame comes from its anatomy (ahead from the heels to the toes), and bones pair up by the side of the body they lie on: Tripo named the ZIN devil's sides the wrong way round for a body facing its toes, and its own clips then turned the hooves half round, crossed the legs and bent the knees backward. Each knee and elbow is then solved again as a two-bone chain on the target's own bones, so it bends the way the source's does (`solve_limb`). On the devil the moved clips' knees went from up to 29% of the leg backward to forward in every clip but one, where the donor's own preset bends a knee back.
3. Check the result as any export (`rig_check.py`, `clip_check.py`), then run the intake.

**A robe that hides the legs.** Tripo's auto-rig looks for legs, and under a floor-length robe it finds none. On the Haiku mage Elzon it put the hips at 0.90 of the height, bound the robe's whole skirt to toe joints at 0.62 and missed the left arm, and a paid re-rig repeats the placement. Repair it free: `py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/robe_rig.py <the rigged export> --out <job folder>/inbox/<asset>__rigged.glb`. It places the joints again from the mesh (the neck where the cross-section narrows under the head, the shoulders below it, each arm from the gap that parts its sleeve from the robe out to the hand's tip, the hidden legs by human proportion), weights the skin again by rule (the robe takes the hips and half of the legs at most, a hanging sleeve never takes the hand), and moves the export's own clips onto the new joints (`lib/tripo_robe_rig.py`). The arms must stand apart from the robe, as Tripo's A pose holds them. Otherwise the tool refuses. Move a made clip's export onto the result with `clip_transfer.py <export> <the re-rigged file> --out <file>`, then check every result with `rig_check.py` and `clip_check.py`. On Elzon the re-rig passed `rig_check.py` and all nine of Tripo's clips passed `clip_check.py` (1.4 to 4.9% of edges stretched). On Tripo's own rig six of the nine failed.

**Tripo's newer rig.** Since November 2026 Tripo rigs new assets with its own skeleton (`BoneRoot`, `Hip`, `Pelvis`, `Waist`, `L_Thigh`, `L_Upperarm`, twist and share bones, a facial bone) instead of Mixamo's. `lib/tripo_rig.py` names both, and the tools that read a rigged GLB before Blender (`lib/glb_root.py`, `lib/tripo_loop.py`, `clip_check.py`, `death_pick.py`, `pose_check.py`) go through it. On the zombie's, the orc's and the old demon's Mixamo GLBs their results are unchanged. A GLB without a hip bone is refused loudly: the travel check once read no hips and passed every clip. The Blender stage (`prepare_tripo_unit.py`) and the grip, shield and hand stages still read Mixamo's names only, so an asset on the newer rig cannot go the route yet. What they need is in `lib/tripo_rig.py`: the bone map, the in-between bones whose motion is composed onto their children before they are dropped (`compose_basis`), and the names for the Blender stages (`BoneNamer`).

## Pick the clips by measurement

The agent picks one clip per role by these rules, and records every candidate's numbers in the job. `clip_check.py` gives stretch and ground. Spin, hand reach, pelvis travel and the tumble test are read from the clip's joint positions in the exported GLB. The kit has no single command for them yet, so the agent computes them and records how. `TRIPO-NOT-DOWN` and limb-in-torso run in `tripo_checks.py` on the exported unit, so a death candidate is confirmed there after the run.

A death can be picked before the run with `tripo/death_pick.py`, once an earlier run of the same unit has exported its mesh. It plays each candidate of the rigged GLB on that mesh, whose weights are the route's, and measures the thigh split (TRIPO-DEATH-SPLAY), the hover (TRIPO-DEATH-HOVER), the end pose (TRIPO-NOT-DOWN, TRIPO-DEATH-REST), a leg kicking up after the body lies down (TRIPO-DEATH-LEG-KICK), limb-in-torso and the kit's deformed edges. With `--settled` it measures each candidate again after the route's death settles, as the run will export it, and picks on those numbers.

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/death_pick.py <job folder>/inbox/<asset>__rigged.glb <earlier run's unit folder> --clips defeat_02 defeat_03 fall "<first words of a made clip>" --settled --json <job folder>/evidence/death_pick.json
```

1. Every candidate must pass the stretch, spin and ground checks, and a death must pass limb-in-torso.
2. **Pose on the route's skin.** When an earlier run of the same body exists, run `tripo/pose_check.py <rigged.glb> <earlier run's .mesh>`. It plays every clip of the GLB on that run's skeleton and weights and reports the shoulder swell (TRIPO-SHOULDER-SWELL), the girdle's tear (TRIPO-SHOULDER-TEAR), the neck (TRIPO-NECK-SUNK), the holding hand's raise above the chest, each hand's reach ahead of the chest, the hips' travel and the strike (the striking hand's travel against the chest and its peak speed, TRIPO-ATTACK-NO-STRIKE). Name the attack candidates with `--attack` (and `--unarmed` for a unit without a weapon): they must strike as well, and the tool prints the pick of step 3. It gives each number for the clip as Tripo made it and after the route's neck lift, shoulder ease and clavicle lever. An attack or a defend must pass the swell, the tear and the neck after the route's rules. Prefer the one whose tear after the rules is nearest vanilla's worst (0.125): reviewers saw the ogre's armpit torn at 0.155 though the check passed. The weights are the earlier run's and the grip stage is not run, so the numbers are an estimate: on the round 4 ogre the exported clips measured as there within 0.003 before the rules, and the route's own ease went further than the estimate on the defend (0.058 against 0.103).
3. **Attack.** It must strike: the striking hand (the weapon's, or the faster hand of an unarmed unit) travels at least 0.25 of the body's height against the chest over the clip and reaches 1.0 heights a second, further and faster than any vanilla infantry attack moves a hand while it aims and fires. Among those that pass the pose step and strike, the largest raise of the holding hand or forward reach of the hands (wrist ahead of the chest, as a share of the body's height) plus the travel of the pelvis. It must be at least 0.2. Reach alone picked a lunge that holds both arms out for the whole clip three times, and each review found no strike. On the zombie (2026-11-18, on the skin of zombie_o) the lunge (travel 0.11), `Classic zombie attack` (0.03) and the clawing clip (0.12) fail the strike. `box_01` (0.26, 1.5 a second, reach 0.36), the lurching swipe (0.33, reach 0.29) and `front_kick_01` (0.57, reach 0.22) pass,. Neither may be picked: under D-127 a boxing or kick preset never fits a unit, so the card no longer offers them and the intake refuses a job that names one.
4. **Death.** It must end lying down, on the front or the back (TRIPO-NOT-DOWN), and must not tumble: the feet never above the head, the pelvis never turned past 120 degrees. With an earlier run's mesh, `death_pick.py --settled` decides: the candidate that passes every death limit with the fewest deformed edges. Without one, the lowest limb-in-torso wins. The intake also refuses a role clip whose hips travel more than 0.1 of the hip height (Animation in Place). A death is judged against vanilla's deaths instead, which move the pelvis 1.31 hip heights as the body falls (limit 1.64, `lib/glb_root.py`). Before that rule, on the demon (2026-11-18) `defeat_03` (0.13) and the zombie stagger clip (0.13) were refused, so of its deaths only `fall` (0.096) was left, and `death_pick.py --settled` passed it. The run (demon_a) then failed it: the body lands on its side, one wing stands straight up and the chest rests 19.6% of the height up (TRIPO-DEATH-REST), the waist turns 80 degrees (TRIPO-WAIST-TWIST) and the wing membrane stretches 14% (TRIPO-WING-STRETCH). death_pick plays the clip without the wing chains' keys, so a winged unit's death is confirmed only by the run. On the zombie (2026-11-18) only the text-to-motion death passed every limit once settled (661 deformed edges, defeat_03 325 but split 93 degrees). defeat_02 never lies down, and fall splits the thighs 56 degrees.
5. **Defend and retreat.** The lowest stretch among the candidates that pass. A defend must read as a guard, not a second attack: its holding hand stays below the chest's height plus a tenth of the body and it has no fast strike. A retreat must stride: name the move and retreat candidates with `pose_check.py --gait`. It prints the share of the whole clip that stands, the loop window the route would cut, and the window's slowest stride and seam speed change, and it fails a clip with no window (TRIPO-LOOP-STANDS). A flinch or a guard is not a retreat.
6. **Idle and move.** `idle` and `walk`, unless they fail a check.
7. **When no candidate passes a role,** use the fixed default for the body type: idle `idle`, move `walk`, defend `hit_to_stomach`, retreat `flee_01`, death `defeat_03` (the tile labelled `defeat_02`). An attack has no free default, because no free unarmed attack fits a unit (D-127). Then make one text-to-motion clip for the role, written to fit the unit.

On the round 4 ogre (2026-11-18, on the skin of its run ogre_k) no attack clip passed as Tripo made it. After the route's rules the chop still swelled (0.097, limit 0.0775), and four text-to-motion clips passed. The one with the largest raise (`A heavy warrior stands holding a long poleaxe upright...`, raise 0.32, reach 0.33) became the attack, and `hit_to_stomach` (0.057) the defend, in place of `hit_to_body_01` (0.103). The run with them (ogre_l) passed every Tripo check: on the exported files the attack swelled 0.059 and the defend 0.060, and the lowest neck was 0.84. The attack raises the club over the head and brings it down, and the defend is a flinch with the free arm across the body.

For a death, `tripo/death_pick.py <rigged.glb> <earlier run's mesh> --clips ... --settled` plays each candidate on that run's skin with the route's death settles and names the pick. On the round 4 ogre none passed: `defeat_02` and the four text clips do not lie down (TRIPO-NOT-DOWN), `defeat_03` splits its thighs 88 degrees after the settles (TRIPO-DEATH-SPLAY, limit 47.9), and `fall` came closest at 48.0, with 827 stretched edges in its worst frame.

**Read at map size.** `pose_check.py --map` also gives each clip's TRIPO-MAP-READ reading against the idle, as made and after the route's rules, and leaves an attack under the limit out of the pick. `--trim CLIP=FRAMES` cuts a clip as the job's `tripo.trim` does, so the idle can be cut as the run cuts it. An attack and a defend must reach the limits. On zombie_w's skin (2026-11-18) the recoil defend read 106 pixels as Tripo made it and 104 exported, so the route did not weaken it: the clip recoils for half a second and stands for four. box_01 read 166, the swipe 190 and the lurch 181, all under 300, and `front_kick_01` 517.

Whether an attack reads as an attack stays a judgement. A clip that passes every number can still look like a guard. The fresh reviewer decides. If the review fails the attack, make one text-to-motion clip (20 credits). If that fails too, stop and ask.

### Secondary chains: cloaks and wings

Tripo's humanoid rig has no cloth or wing bones. It binds a long cloak to the thighs, and a wing membrane to the arms and shoulders. A fur cloak or mantle down the back counts as a cloak: set `"cape": true` and the `cloak` chain. Without them the round 4 dwarf's fur was bound to the clavicles down to the knees, so it ballooned with the raised arm (the glaive in the attack, frame 50), and neither TRIPO-CLOAK-STRETCH (6.5% there, limit 5%) nor the cloak's own rules ran. With both set (dwarf_g) the shoulder swelling fell (attack 8.4% to 5.2%), but the chained fur still stretched past TRIPO-CLOAK-STRETCH in the attack, the defend (5.1%) and the retreat (9.1%), went 5% into the body in the death, and in the retreat hung below the boots, so the grounding lifted the feet 7.4% of the height (UNIT-ANIM-GROUND). A cloak that reaches the knees on a short body is not yet handled by the chain rules. Add `"chains"` to the job's `tripo` block and the intake gives that region its own bone chains, rebinds it and keys it in every role clip by fixed rules:

```
"tripo": {"roles": {...}, "chains": [{"region": "cloak"}]}
"tripo": {"roles": {...}, "chains": [{"region": "wings"}]}
```

Nothing in the job sets a number. The stage reads the body and follows these rules (`lib/tripo_chains.py`, run in Blender by `tripo/chain_stage.py`):

| Step | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Region | Cloak: the region TRIPO-CLOAK-STRETCH measures, grown along the mesh into the cloth beside it that Tripo bound to the torso, hips or thighs, behind the hips and off every limb. Wings: everything farther from the mid-plane than 0.18 of the unit's width, without the forearms and hands, left and right |
| Bone budget | HOI4 allows 50 bones. What the kit's bones leave is shared equally by the regions' sides. A pair of wings is two sides |
| Chain shape | A chain bone is half a thigh long. Bones per chain are the region's length over that, 2 to 4. Chains are its width over that, 2 to 6. Over budget, bones per chain drop to 3 first, then chains to 2 |
| Placement | Cloak: an even fan of chains across its width, each hanging from the upper spine. Wings: a fan from the wing root at the upper back, each chain moved onto a finger ridge where the wing has one |
| Weights | Each vertex takes two neighbouring chains and two bones of each, so at most 4 influences. The region takes the chains whole. The chain weights blend into the body's over one chain bone outward from the region's border, and over at least 6 times the region's thickness (`lib/tripo_chains.region_thickness`: the median extent along the sheet's normal in cells of 3% of the height) |
| Cloak motion | All chains move as one sheet. Each bone follows the upper spine at the top and the hips at the hem, through a spring at the swing rate of a pendulum as long as the bone. The sheet is pushed off the legs and torso along the hips' back direction, never points above the horizontal, and stays above the ground |
| Wing motion | A fold pose per role and a flap: a sine on each bone, a little later down the chain. Idle folded and still with a slow breath, move half spread with a beat per step, attack spread with one strong beat, defend wrapped forward round the body, retreat folded along the back with the head looking back (round g), death folding first and sagging flat with the body. The attack and the retreat turn their face to the review cameras frame by frame. A folded fan keeps 70% of its spread so the membrane never squashes |
| Loops | Idle and move end where they start |

The chains are keyed before the snap easing, so a bone a collision turned fast is eased like any other joint. The intake report lists the plan under `chains`: the bones, the region sizes and each role's fastest chain turn.

Three checks cover the regions. TRIPO-WING-STRETCH is TRIPO-CLOAK-STRETCH for wings. TRIPO-CHAIN-IN-BODY fails a clip in which more than 1% of the cloak or wing points sit deeper than 1% of the height inside the rest of the body. TRIPO-CHAIN-GROUND fails one in which more than 1% of them sink 1% of the height under the ground. They run for `--cape` or `--wings`, or for a job whose `tripo.chains` names the region. TRIPO-CLOAK-STRETCH counts the cloak's chain bones as cloak bones.

For a winged unit, set `target_height_m` so that the body without the wings is a soldier's height.

**Armour plates.** Add `{"region": "plates"}` to `chains` when the unit wears plates over its shoulders and chest. Tripo splits a plate's weight across the joint it covers, so a pauldron bends whenever the arm lifts. The stage finds the plates by rule from the rig's joints. A pauldron is the surface within 60% of the upper arm's length of the shoulder joint, outside it, and no lower than 20% of that length under it. A chest plate is the surface ahead of the upper spine, between the middle spine and the neck, inside the shoulders. Plates that touch form one assembly, so no seam between them tears. Each assembly is bound wholly to the bone nearest the root of those that hold at least a tenth of its weight. The weight blends back into the body over a ring as wide as the assembly, and the ring stops before any other plate. Plates take no bones. They are bound after the chains, so a chain's blend never reaches into a plate. TRIPO-PLATE-STRETCH fails a plate that stretches more than vanilla clothing does at its worst, 2%.

**What the chains keep clear of.** The chains are kept out of capsules round the legs, the torso, the head and the arms. A chain's blend never reaches a forearm, hand, shin or foot: the share fades out before them over one chain bone.

**Wing room.** A wing's poses are capped by the body:

- A fold swings it back until it lies along the back.
- Every pose swings it back at least to the side floor: the least swing past which swinging further hides little more of the body from the side (within 2% of the body of a full swing). A spread wing lies behind the shoulders and from the side covers the back of the shoulder and the arm. On the demon the floor is 37 degrees.
- When the torso turns, the wing on the side that comes forward swings back by the turn as well, because the review cameras stay put.
- A down-stroke stops when the lowest chain hangs straight down.
- A wing never holds a dying body up. The death settles and the ground pass leave the wing out, and a wing that then reaches under the ground is turned up about its root (see Wings of a death off the ground in [Tripo defaults](#tripo-defaults)).
- A death folds the wings fully in the first 15% of the clip, or by the fall if that comes sooner. The fall starts when the hips drop 5% of the height. While the torso still stands, each wing is lifted in its own plane until its lowest chain stays above both knees, so a kneeling body has no wing across its legs. Only after the fall starts does the wing sag in its own plane until its highest chain lies level, and the back swing lasts only while the torso stands, so the wings lie flat beside a fallen body.
- Each bone adds 0.3 of its parent's flap, so the tips do not curl into spikes.
- No bone of a standing wing points more than 10 degrees above its own rest elevation in the world. A capped bone turns down in its own vertical plane and keeps its heading. A swept-back wing rises when the torso leans over it: the demon's box_01 leans the upper spine 24 degrees forward and 22 sideways at the punch, and both wings' mean direction rose from 16 to 44 degrees above level, hit_to_body_01 to 40 and flee_01 to 41. At map size each read as a wing stood upright, and a fresh review failed the attack for it. Capped (replayed on run demon_a), the peaks fell to 17, 23 and 18 degrees, with the side cover, the face shares and the root stretch unchanged within a point. A dying wing keeps its own rules.
- **Feathered wings are slabs.** A wing thicker than 4% of the unit's height (feathers laid over each other: 0.073 on the ZIN angel, against 0.014 to 0.016 on the demon's and the devil's membranes) moves whole, and three rules follow. The border blend runs over 6 times its thickness: on the angel one chain bone left 19.9% of the root's edges stretched in the idle's fold, 6.2%, 2.2% and 4.1% at 2, 3 and 5 times that bone (`tripo/chain_replay.py` on run angel_a, 2026-11-23). The 10 degree rise cap is kept by one rigid turn for the whole wing (`lib/tripo_chains.tilt_limited_carry`): the wing follows the torso's lean by the largest share (steps of 5%) at which no bone rises past its cap, keeping the torso's heading, and when the lean is gone and the wing's own up-stroke still passes the cap, every bone's up-stroke shrinks by one share. The cap turned down bone by bone tore the angel's wing (the attack stretched 15.8% of its edges, 0.7% with no cap, 6.5% with the rigid turn on the old slash, which the made strike replaces). No bone is pushed off the ground one by one: the angel's death stretched 10.2% of the wing's edges with the push and 0.7% without it, and the ground pass turns a wing that reaches under the ground up about its root
- A beating wing (the attack and the retreat) holds its face toward the review cameras, which stay put while the torso turns and leans under it. Each frame takes the back swing and the tilt of the top back that show the wing's face most to the side views and to the map camera on either side of the unit. The swing runs from the side floor plus the torso's twist up to 70 degrees, the tilt up to 40 degrees, and the turns are smoothed over 9 frames. The map camera stands on both sides because the review sheets render the scene while `tripo/tripo_checks.py` reads the exported `.mesh` in its own axes, which io_pdx_mesh writes as the scene's mirror image. The check's left view is the sheets' right view.
- Only the chain bones past the root take that face turn. The root bone keeps the role's own turn, so the skin where the wing grows from the shoulders turns no further than before.
- A bone past the root of such a wing takes the pose's whole rotation, roll included. Turned by the shortest arc onto its direction alone, neighbouring chains rolled apart under the swing and tilt and twisted the membrane out of the wing's plane.

**Wings at the game camera (D-83).** The game shows a unit from the front and above, like the review sheets' hoi4 view (25 degrees round, 55 down). From there a wing swung back stands up the screen as a spike, even when it does not rise. The side views are close-ups, so for the attack and the defend the game camera decides:

1. TRIPO-WING-SCREEN reports, as advice since D-140, a clip with spread wings (every role but the folded idle and the death) in which a wing's mean direction from its root rises more than 56 degrees above the screen's level in the map view, on either side of the unit, in any judged frame. The bound is 60 degrees: the highest angle fresh reviewers saw at game size without naming the wing, with a twentieth added. 2. The attack and the defend aim their wings at that camera (`tripo/chain_stage.screen_shares`). It keeps the largest share under which the chains' mean direction from the root stays at or under 51 degrees, the move's own. Past 0 it also takes away the beat's up-stroke, down to none at -1. When no share gets there, it keeps the one with the lowest angle. The share changes by at most 0.1 a frame. The posed chains carry the flap and the torso's own turn and lean, so both are counted. ### A death that burns away

A job may choose another kind of death: `"death_kind": "burn"` in its `tripo` block. The unit does not fall and lie. It is struck, burns where it stands and leaves a small heap of ash. The demon dies this way (maintainer decision D-85). The death role's clip is then a standing reaction, such as `hit_to_stomach`, and the route makes it a burn on the exported files (`lib/tripo_burn.py`):

1. The reaction plays to its peak, the frame farthest from its first pose, and holds there, so the body never stands up again.
2. From the peak the body burns away by bone scale over 2.5 seconds, broken up so it never reads as a whole miniature (an even shrink left a half-size demon standing in the fire, run demon_d). The upper arms, the thighs and the wing roots shrink first and fastest, to 0.15 of their size by half the burn. The torso and the head start a quarter into the burn and end at 0.07 of the size, shrinking toward the ground point under the feet. The hips sink so the lowest point of the body stays on the ground while the legs shrink under it.
3. An ash heap, a low wide mound (six rings of 24 points, flat on top and falling off at a ragged rim, its height 0.16 of its radius, so it reads as a heap from above and not as a ball) on its own bone `Ash` under the hips, grows from the feet from 15% of the burn on, as the body goes, and stays for a second. In every other clip the bone keeps its rest pose, where the heap is bound at a thousandth of its size just inside the skin by the hips, so it is never seen. The heap is its own stream of the unit's shape with its own ash material: `<stem>_ash_diffuse.dds` (dark grey to black grain with a faint ember tint), a flat normal map (blue black, since blue is emissive) and a matte specular map, all DXT5 with mips. A fresh review read the first heap, three rings of ten points textured from the unit's own darkest texels, as a hard seven-sided slab of skin, a tile and not ash. The heap is new geometry with its own texels. The unit's own texture stays as Tripo made it and is never darkened or tinted to show burning (D-86). The burning reads from the particles and the shrink.
4. A locator `burn`, parented to `Spine02`, carries the fire and the smoke. `wire_unit.py` writes two particle events into the entity's death state at time 0, and two mod particle files. These are copies of vanilla's `building_fire` and `city_smoke` whose endless subsystems emit only for the burn (the fire) and a second past the clip (the smoke), plus a `.gfx` file that registers them.
5. The wings keep the idle's folded pose in this death (`--chain-role death=idle`), because the body does not fall. The defend's wrapped wings hid 54% of the limbs from the side (run demon_e).

To give any unit a burn death, follow these steps:

1. Pick a standing reaction for the death role: a hit that doubles the body over and stays on its feet (`hit_to_stomach`, or a text-to-motion hit). Its hips must not travel more than 0.1 hip heights (the intake refuses it otherwise).
2. In the job's `tripo` block set `"death_kind": "burn"` and `"roles": {"death": "<that clip>"}`. Nothing else in the job changes.
3. Run the route as usual (`tripo_intake.py`, then `run_pilot.py`). The heap, its bone, the locator and the burnt death are made on the staged files. Check that `runtime/files` holds `<stem>_burn.json` beside the `.mesh`.
4. Run `tripo/tripo_checks.py` with the job. The BURN rules below replace the lying-body rules for that death. Every BURN finding must be 0. When a rule can only pass by cutting something from the unit (a wing, a part, detail), stop and report what would be lost (D-87, D-88).
5. Render the review sheets (`review_unit.py`, `tripo/weapon_sheets.py --sheet game`, `tripo/clip_sheet.py --roles death`). The death's tiles show the fire and the smoke as a stand-in, stamped "particles: stand-in". Judge the game_size sheets and the hoi4 tiles: the body must shrink where it stands under the fire and leave the heap, never vanish at once or lie down.
6. Run `wire_unit.py` on the mod. It writes the two particle events into the entity's death state and the files `gfx/particles/<stem>_fire.asset`, `gfx/particles/<stem>_smoke.asset` and `gfx/entities/<stem>_burn_particles.gfx`. The game must be installed, since the particles are copied from its `gfx/particles/environment/building_fire.asset` and `city_smoke.asset`.
7. Ask for the in-game checks listed at the end of this section.

What vanilla shows that this rests on:

- **Particles in a death state.** GER rifle infantry's death state fires `blood_particle` at its `head` locator at `time = 0`. Ship deaths fire `ship_explosion_particle` at `hit_effect_locator` locators, and plane crashes fire `explosion_particle`.
- **Locators follow a bone.** `GER_infantry.mesh` parents its `head` locator to the bone `head`.
- **Bone scale hides parts.** In `landmark_hakko_ichiu_destroyed_destroy.anim` the bone `hide` scales from 1.0 to 0.07, while the landmark's `destroy` state fires timed explosion and smoke particles. The kit's exports already key `tqs` for every joint.

The kit writes and reads the PDX files itself for this (`lib/pdx_write.py`, which gives the same bytes as the files it reads). The lying-body rules (TRIPO-NOT-DOWN, TRIPO-DEATH-REST, TRIPO-DEATH-FEET, TRIPO-DEATH-HOVER, TRIPO-DEATH-LEG-KICK, TRIPO-DEATH-SPLAY) are not run on this death. Its own rules run instead, in `tripo/tripo_checks.py`:

| Rule | Fails when |
| --- | --- |
| BURN-STANDS | The torso leans more than 60 degrees from upright in any frame (the kit's untwist reads a torso past 60 degrees as not standing) |
| BURN-IN-PLACE | The hips travel more than 0.1 hip heights, the in-place limit of the intake |
| BURN-SHRINK | After the burn starts, the body's top, its outline in the map view or the hips' scale grows again by more than 1% between two frames |
| BURN-GROUNDED | The lowest point goes more than 1% of the height off or under the ground in any frame |
| BURN-NO-FLYOFF | A part moves more than 1% of the height farther from the burn's ground point between two frames |
| BURN-END | In the last 15 frames the body is over 0.07 of its size, or the heap is not grown, not on the ground or wider than the standing body |
| BURN-PARTICLE | The mesh lacks the locator, or the fire does not start by the burn or the smoke does not last past the clip |
| BURN-WINGS | A wing is larger in scale than the body during the burn |

The kit's deformation checks (`check_unit_files.py`) and the other Tripo checks read this death up to the burn's start, since after it the pose is held and only scaled.

The review renders draw the fire and the smoke as a stand-in: orange puffs that rise fast and fade, and grey ones that rise slowly and grow, at the locator, timed as the plan says. Each tile that shows them is stamped "particles: stand-in", because only the game draws the real particles. These things are left for an in-game check:

- How large the fire and the smoke are against the unit at map zoom (both are registered at scale 1).
- How long the game holds the death's last frame before it removes the unit, and so how long the heap shows.

### A defend that reads as a guard

At game size a defend must read as a guard and must differ from the attack. A fresh review of the demon (run demon_c) read its defend, `hit_to_body_01`, as its attack: the same twisted torso, one arm thrust out, no guard. To give a unit a guard:

1. Measure the rigged GLB's clips with `tripo/pose_check.py`. A clip that raises both hands before the face (the raise of both hands near the head's height) and does not twist is the defend. On the demon no clip did: `hit_to_body_01` and `hit_to_stomach` raise a hand 0.05 of the height above the chest.
2. When none does, set `"defend_pose": "guard"` in the job's `tripo` block and keep the clip that reads best as a hit. On the staged files the route then poses the guard by rule (`lib/tripo_guard.py`), before the weights are fitted. The torso turns toward the idle's first pose, so the twist goes, and leans back 10 degrees. Each upper arm points forward, down and out, each forearm up and in, so both hands come up before the face. It all blends in over the first quarter of the clip and out over the last, so the loop does not pop, and the hips and legs keep the clip's own recoil.
3. Run `tripo/tripo_checks.py`. TRIPO-MAP-READ compares the attack and the defend with the idle, and TRIPO-MAP-ALIKE compares the defend with the attack on the map camera, in pixels at game size. Its limit, 125, is the lowest reading of the humanoids fresh reviewers passed (orc_k 407, ogre_l 129, dwarf_f 508). On a winged unit the wings fill most of the silhouette, so the pixels can pass a defend that a reviewer reads as the attack: the demon_c defend read 404. Look at the defend's hoi4 tiles yourself, beside the attack's.
4. The wings stay at the defend's own pose. Bringing them forward round the body as a cloak is not a rule yet.

## Tripo checks

`tripo/tripo_checks.py` reads the unit's runtime files and fails what a viewer fails on a Tripo unit. Each limit is measured on vanilla clips or on clips a reviewer failed.

The map-read rules for animations (TRIPO-MAP-READ, TRIPO-MAP-ALIKE, TRIPO-MAP-POSTURE, TRIPO-MAP-SPREAD, TRIPO-MAP-REACH, TRIPO-MAP-SAME, TRIPO-WING-IDLE-MOVE and TRIPO-WING-SCREEN) are advice since D-140. They are reported under `advisory` and never fail a unit, because a clip is judged at the size of the game's designer preview and in close-up. Their limits below say what each reading means.

| Rule | Fails when |
| --- | --- |
| TRIPO-HANDS-HIGH | In an unarmed unit's attack, move or retreat a hand is above the head in more than 20% of frames, or above the shoulders in more than 80%. From the front a walk with the hands over the head reads as hands up |
| TRIPO-MAP-READ | From the map camera at game size (the unit 60 pixels tall), a humanoid's attack or defend changes fewer pixels of the idle's silhouette than 300 (attack) or 160 (defend) in its 90th percentile frame, so a pose held a tenth of the clip counts and one frame does not. The count is in pixels, not a share of the idle's area, because a slender body has a small area. The limits are the lowest readings of the humanoids fresh reviewers passed (orc_k attack 441 and defend 453, ogre_l 320 and 166, dwarf_f 530 and 493). zombie_w's attack (192) and defend (104), read as its idle by two reviewers, fail. Its recoil peaks at 232 for a tenth of a 5 second clip (`lib/map_read.py`) |
| TRIPO-MAP-POSTURE | The idle's body outline (the skin of every bone but the arms, a weapon, a shield and wings) stands, on the map camera, more than 0.93 of the rest pose's height for a job whose `tripo.posture` is `hunched` (or whose `idle_pose` is `hunch`), or 0.93 or less for an upright one. A hunched body is lower from above, because the head comes toward the camera. The hunch rule reads 0.88 (runs f and h, read as hunched by four reviewers), vanilla's GER infantry idle 0.98, the passed humanoids 0.98 to 1.02, and run d's upright idle, which reviewers read as a live soldier, 1.00 (`lib/map_outline.py`) |
| TRIPO-MAP-SPREAD | A humanoid's attack or defend holds its hands 0.80 of the idle body's height apart or more on the map camera, one each side of the chest, in its 90th percentile frame: it reads as a T-pose or a flail. Run h's wide grab read 0.87 and its recoil defend 1.21, both read as a T-pose by two reviewers. The passed humanoids read 0.70 at most (orc_k's defend, both hands on the haft), vanilla's GER infantry 0.24 |
| TRIPO-MAP-REACH | At a melee attack's strike (the frames where the striking end reaches within 0.02 of the height of its furthest ahead of the chest), the striking end shows less than 5 pixels outside the body's outline on the map camera. The striking end is the weapon's skin, or the middle of each hand's skin for an unarmed unit. From the high front camera a forward reach comes toward the viewer and lands on the body and legs. Runs f and w read 4, the passed weapons 9 (ogre_l's club) to 29 (dwarf_f's glaive). A winged unit's reach is not judged, because its wings carry the attack (demon_g's claws read 0 and two reviewers passed it). Vanilla's infantry attacks shoot and are no reference |
| TRIPO-MAP-SAME | The attack and the defend both fail TRIPO-MAP-SPREAD: they read as each other at map size (run h). It names the defend |
| TRIPO-LOOP-POP | The last frame of an idle, move, attack, defend or retreat clip is more than 8 degrees from its first at some joint. Vanilla's role clips measure 0.0 to 6.3, the orc's attack measured 119 |
| TRIPO-LOOP-STANDS | In a humanoid's move or retreat the feet stop striding in some frame step, or the last step does not flow into the first. The stride speed is how fast one ankle moves against the other, as a share of the height a second, smoothed over a sixth of a second around the loop. It must stay at 0.14 or more: vanilla's eight looped locomotion clips (GER infantry moving, march, retreat and charge) never drop under 0.179, and its idle and standing attack never reach 0.11. Across the seam no joint's speed against the pelvis may change by more than 1.2 times the largest change between two steps inside the clip (vanilla's worst seam is 0.96 of it, the retreat). A sprint changes its joints' speeds several times as hard as a march from step to step, so the seam is judged against the clip itself. The zombie's `flee_01` retreat stood in 14% of its steps, the ogre's in 8%, the orc's in 28% and the dwarf's in 21%, and the dwarf's and the orc's walks stood for a few steps where a settle eased them into the first pose. The loop window fixes it (see [Tripo defaults](#tripo-defaults)) |
| TRIPO-FOOT-BELOW-GROUND | A foot or toe sinks more than 0.1% of the height under the ground in a clip that is not a death. `UNIT-ANIM-GROUND` reads the toes too, but its limit is vanilla's worst clip of all (4.2%, a push-up), and vanilla's own role clips sink at most 0.9% |
| TRIPO-DEATH-FEET | In the last 15 frames of a death the boots hang more than 1% of the height above the ground. Vanilla's deaths end within 0.6% |
| TRIPO-DEATH-HOVER | A death holds its hips up while it falls. The body's support (the pelvis and the legs) is measured per frame as the lowest height over its skin, a point far from the pelvis counting as higher by how far it lies beyond the hip width, so a foot far in front of the hips does not hold them up. A span of at least 4 frames before the body lies, whose support peaks 8% or more above the ground and after which the pelvis has come down (a sag and a stand-up is a stagger, not a hold: the zombie's defeat_03 dips 10% and reads 2.16), fails when its length is more than 2.12 times the time a free fall takes to drop that peak. Vanilla's falls (GER infantry death_mg, death_rifle) measure 1.66 and 1.3 times. The zombie's "invisible chair" measured 6.0, the demon kneeling on its wing tips 2.5. `lib/tripo_fall.py` also settles it: the pelvis follows a free fall from the speed it had, never by more than the gap and never through the ground, and each planted foot stays where it is while the thigh and knee fold |
| TRIPO-DEATH-REST | A death ends with the hips on the ground and the chest, the head or a hand more than 10.2% of the height above it. Vanilla's deaths end with every part within 8.2% |
| TRIPO-DEATH-SPLAY | In some frame of a humanoid's death the two thighs (each from its hip joint to its knee joint) are more than 47.9 degrees apart, or, for a body whose rest pose holds them wider, more than its own rest split plus 24.8. The crotch and the raised thigh stretch into one mass. Vanilla's two deaths split them 38.3 degrees at most (GER infantry, the same skeleton on four meshes), 19.8 past its rest pose's 18.5, and each limit adds a quarter. The round 4 ogre's rest pose holds its thick thighs 33.0 apart and its clips stand at 48 to 51, so its limit is 57.8. The zombie (rest 12.2) and the orc (20.8) keep 47.9 Region stretch shares cannot tell this: vanilla's low-poly legs deform far more by any of them (a crouch, the push-up). ## Armed units

Tripo's rig bends a weapon modelled into the hand, and Tripo's clips animate an empty-handed body. So the body is modelled with empty hands, the weapon is its own model, and the kit puts the weapon in the hand, closes the fingers around it (`tripo/grip_weapon.py`) and keeps it out of the body by turning the wrist. An orc with a poleaxe passed two fresh reviews this way in November 2026, and a dwarf with a glaive and a shield passed too (it counts after a run from its full picture, see the top of this page). No weaker model has run the armed route from the card alone yet.

**The armed route, in order.** `tripo/make_card.py` writes these steps into the card with the job's names filled in.

1. In the job's `tripo.weapon`, write `hand` by the rig's side: the picture is a front view, so a weapon on the viewer's right is in the `LeftHand`. Every stage defaults to `RightHand`, so a job without `hand` is refused by `make_card.py` and `arm_unit.py`. Write `name`, and `grip_options` with `length_ratio` and `grip` read from the art.
2. Redraw the body with empty hands (step 1 of the route, ending `Remove the <weapon> completely`), and redraw the weapon alone from the same art: `Redraw only the <weapon> from this picture for 3D modelling. Keep its exact shape, proportions, materials, colours and every mark. Remove the hand and everything else. Invent nothing. The whole <weapon> standing upright, head at the top, seen from the side that shows the head's shape, on a plain light grey background, even soft lighting.`
3. Make the body model (30), then the weapon model (30) as a new asset with the same settings. Export the weapon on its Model page, GLB 2K, as `<job folder>/armed/<asset>_weapon__model.glb`. A shield goes the same way as `<asset>_shield__model.glb`.
4. Rig the body (20) and apply the candidates. The free library has no overhead weapon blow: searches for `attack`, `smash`, `swing`, `slam` and `hammer` find nothing (2026-11-22), and the boxing and kick presets never fit a unit (D-127). Apply `chop` and every earlier text-to-motion clip made for a weapon in one hand (they cost nothing). For a hammer, a club, a mallet or an axe swung overhead, plan one text-to-motion attack (20) from the start, and name the hand by the character's own side (`in his own left hand` for the `LeftHand`).
5. Export the body as `<job folder>/armed/<asset>__body.glb`, and run `clip_check.py ... --attack chop <earlier clips>` before you leave Tripo (step 7 of the route).
6. Pick the clips (step 7) and write them into `tripo.roles`.
7. Run `py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/arm_unit.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> <unit>`. It runs the grip stage with the job's hand, the weapon on its own `Weapon` bone, the hold in idle, move, defend and retreat, the hold fading as the attack starts, the drop at the death's fall and the clearance in every role, then the shield stage for the other hand when the job has `tripo.shield`. It writes `<job folder>/inbox/<asset>__rigged.glb` and the stage reports in `<job folder>/evidence`. `--dry-run` prints the commands and starts nothing.
8. Go on with the intake (step 8), `run_pilot.py` and the checks, as for any unit.

What the route still does not do well:

- The rule keeps the weapon out of the body in idle, move, defend and retreat without changing how they read.
- An attack made for an empty hand does not become a weapon swing. With the weapon cleared from the body it reads as a stiff hold. A weapon attack needs a clip made for the weapon.
- A long weapon carried down in a death clip passes through the body or the ground.
- A coarse Tripo hand does not wrap a haft tightly.

**The clearance follows the cloak.** A cloak that gets its own bone chains (`tripo.chains`) swings a few percent of the height off the rigid pose the grip stage clears the weapon in, and the swing can carry cloth through a weapon that only just cleared it (the orc's retreat, 2.2% of the weapon's points inside the body in 8 frames, all of them against the cloak). The clearance rule keeps the weapon `--soft-share` (4% of the height) clear of the cloak's vertices as well, wherever the body has a cloak by the check's own region rule, with no job setting. The check on the exported files stays the judge: a frame fails the grip stage only by the check's own test.

**The head leads the blow.** The haft lies across the palm and the head can point toward the index finger or toward the little finger, the same line in the same fist. `grip_weapon.py --head-toward auto` carries that line through the attack (`--hold-fade-clips`) after the hold has faded and puts the head on the side that leads the strike, the hand's fastest frame (`lib/tripo_swing.py`). The orc's attack chops down with the hand turned so that an index-side head stays above the hand: the butt led at a cosine of -0.42, and with the axe the other way round in the same fist the head leads at +0.50. A roll of the forearm cannot do it: the head would have to turn 145 to 170 degrees about the forearm at the chop. `auto` is not the default yet. On the orc the other side put the axe 6.3% into the body in the follow-through, beyond what the clearance's 60 degree wrist turn clears, and the hold missed its grip point by 11% to 14% of the height instead of 3% to 8%. TRIPO-WEAPON-HEAD-LEAD fails such an attack, so the attack clip itself has to change: a clip made for a one-handed chop.

**The weapon turns in the hand first, the arm last.** A frame the wrist turn cannot clear within its cap gets two more turns, in this order, each only for the frames the turns before it left in the body (`lib/tripo_clearance.clear_clip_staged`): the roll of the part about its own long axis through the grip (the fist turning the haft or the shield's bar it holds), then the smallest turn of the upper arm about the shoulder, up to 30 degrees. The arm is last because it changes the clip's own motion. It never lowers a hand the clip raises above the shoulder by more than 1% of the height, and never puts the elbow or the wrist inside the body. A roll or an arm turn grows in and fades out by at most 8 degrees a frame, so no joint snaps. The grip stage, the shield stage and the final clearance on the exported files use the same order. The grip stage then eases its turns as the intake does and measures the clip again, and clears again a frame the easing put back (up to three passes, `worst_share_after_easing` in the report): on the ogre's chop a solved frame next to an unsolved one turned the hand 85 degrees in one frame, and the eased clip had the club 29% in the body in frames 68 to 72 while the report said 0.6%. To leave the easing little to retime, the stage first keeps the hand's added turn from jumping between two frames by more than the easing allows, 14 degrees a frame less the clip's own hand turn and at least 4, by the final clearance's bridge (`bridge_turns`, `wrist_room`). The searches past the wrist are bounded by a count of tests of the part against the body: 1,000 for the roll and the arm and 500 for the wrist's bridge per clip in the grip stage, 2,000 per bridge in the final clearance. Past a bound the best turns found so far are kept, the frames still in the body are reported, and `search_bound_reached` says so. Unbounded, the dwarf's grip stage ran past an hour. Each test against one posed body now reuses that body's ray test (`clip_check.InsideTest`), which cut a test from about 60 ms to about 15.

**A raise is kept.** The grip report gives every clip's holding hand at its highest above the chest joint, in the clip as Tripo made it and in the stage's result (`hand_raise`). A clip whose raw hand rises above the shoulder and whose result holds it more than 3% of the height lower fails the stage (exit 3, `RAISE_LOST`). The hold, the easing and the clearance's arm turn all move the arm, and none of them may take a blow's raise away.

**A weapon over the head is not through it.** Two fresh reviewers failed the orc's overhead wind-up for the axe passing through the head. Measured on the exported files it does not: the weapon stays at least 8.5% of the height from the head's skin while it crosses over it, and the clearance rule keeps the head 4% clear as it does the feet. From the front the blade is in front of the face, and from the side the haft crosses the crown, so no view shows the gap. No rule judges that overlap yet.

**A dropped weapon's stillness is judged on the exported files.** The grip stage measures the weapon after landing on its own GLB (11.9% of the height on the orc) and the exported death differs (the retarget, the easing and the pipeline's grounding of each frame), so the stage prints it as a diagnostic and fails only for the weapon inside the body or under the ground. `tripo_checks.py` reports `moved_in_last_half_share` for every clip.

**A clip that ends still is trimmed.** Tripo's text-to-motion attacks run for the length asked and then hold their last pose (the orc's attack chops by frame 80 and stands still to frame 120). The intake cuts the still tail of attack, defend and retreat clips by one rule (`lib/tripo_trim.py`): the motion is the joint turn per frame summed over the body, the floor is 5% of the clip's own peak (its 95th percentile), and the clip keeps half a second after the last frame above the floor. It cuts only when that saves a fifth of the clip and leaves a second. Idle, move and death are never cut by the rule. A job's own `tripo.trim` for a role wins, and `"auto_trim": false` (or a list of roles) switches the rule off.

The weapon checks judge the exported `.mesh` and `.anim` files, the ones the game loads. TRIPO-WEAPON-IN-BODY and TRIPO-WEAPON-BELOW-GROUND are measured there, frame by frame. The grip stage's own GLB is only a diagnostic. In a death clip the weapon is dropped before the body falls: the hand lets go at the start of the fall, and the weapon tumbles to the ground on its own path, flat and clear of where the body will lie. The retarget keeps the weapon bone's location for this. A coarse Tripo hand cannot wrap a haft tightly. A job may set `tripo.weapon.grip_check` to `"advisory"`, so TRIPO-GRIP-ENCLOSURE is reported but does not fail the unit, and a reviewer judges the hand at game zoom. TRIPO-GRIP-THUMB still counts.

An attack made for an empty hand still does not become a weapon swing, so an armed unit needs a clip made for its weapon (step 4 above).

### Shields

A shield held in the free hand is a second rigid part. Model it on its own like the weapon, then run `tripo/shield_stage.py` on the grip stage's GLB, before the intake:

```
blender -b --factory-startup --python .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/shield_stage.py -- <gripped.glb> <shield.glb> --out <shielded.glb> --report <report.json> --hand RightHand --hold-clips <the weapon's hold clips> --hold-fade-clips <attack> --release-clips <death> --clearance-clips <every role clip>
```

The stage runs the grip stage's own steps on the free hand. Only the grip on the shield is its own rule (`lib/tripo_shield.py`), read from the model with no per-unit number:

| Step | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Size | The shield's longest axis is `--height-share` of the body's height, 0.65 by default (the dwarf's art) |
| Outer edge | The side the shield's middle bows to, away from the line between its ends. The spikes of a crescent are there |
| Grip | The fist closes round the middle of the inner edge, on a bar along the long axis just inside the hollow. The bar has a held haft's radius, 1.5% of the height |
| Roll | The shield reaches out of the back of the fist (`--roll 180`), so the palm and the fingers stay outside it and a hanging arm holds the outer edge away from the body |
| Hold | The grip goes to the unit's side with the long axis upright, by default high enough that the lower end is 8% of the height over the ground |
| Binding | Weight 1.0 on its own bone `Shield`, a child of the hand, as mesh `tripo_part_2`. With the weapon the unit has 24 bones of HOI4's 50 |
| Clearance | The weapon's rule: the hand turns about the wrist, frame by frame, until the shield leaves the body and the ground, then the shield rolls about its bar in the fist, then the upper arm turns, never lowering a raised hand. The fist's zone round the grip is not judged. The roll matters most for a shield: a wrist turn swings a crescent round the fist and through the torso again, a roll swings it out |
| Death | The weapon's rule: the fist lets go at the start of the fall and the shield lies flat on the ground clear of the body and the dropped weapon (`--release-clips <death>`). `--fall-fade-clips <death>` keeps it in the fist instead, as vanilla keeps a rifle, and the clearance then judges every frame |

The job names the shield in its `tripo` block. The intake then binds `tripo_part_2` to `Shield` and the weight rules leave the bone alone. In its `release_roles` the bone keeps its location keys and never decides where the ground is, so a shield kept in the fist leaves the list out:

```
"tripo": {"weapon": {...}, "shield": {"part": "tripo_part_2", "bone": "Shield", "release_roles": ["death"]}}
```

`tripo_checks.py` judges it on the exported files with the weapon's two rules, TRIPO-SHIELD-IN-BODY and TRIPO-SHIELD-BELOW-GROUND. Each part's check leaves the other part out of the body.

Tested in November 2026 on the round-4 dwarf (glaive in the left hand, the crescent shield, 0.65 of his height, in the right fist), measured on the exported files by the shield checks:

| Role | Shield kept in the fist, outer edge held out | Dropped, earlier hold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idle, move | 0% | 0% |
| Defend | 1.5% in the body in 2 frames | 2.8% in 77 frames |
| Retreat | 1.3% in 8 frames | 2.3% in 7 frames |
| Attack | 35.7% in 51 frames | 41.7% in 69 frames |
| Death | 5.1% in the body in 2 frames, 3.8% under the ground in 5, and the body ends 56% of its height tall (TRIPO-NOT-DOWN) | 0% and 0%, the body ends 39% tall |

The death rule follows from the last row. Kept in the fist, the shield ends standing on its edge beside the fallen body, and the wrist turn cannot lay it flat. Dropped, it lands flat, 4.3% of the height clear of the body. The outer-edge target of the hold brought the outer edge from 56.5 degrees off the side (the idle) to under 1.5 degrees in every held clip, and the grip moved 8 to 14% of the height from the hold point instead of 5 to 9%. The attack fails as the weapon's does, because Tripo's attack holds both hands on one haft before the body, and needs a clip made for a shield.

The death fall (2026-11-18). The zombie's death held its hips about 17 frames over a seat of air, its feet 27% of the height in front of its pelvis, and the demon's knelt on its wing tips. The body's lowest vertex is on the ground in both, so the old checks passed them. TRIPO-DEATH-HOVER measures the pelvis and the legs and settles the hips in a free fall. On the zombie the span fell from 6.0 times a free fall to 1.6, with the feet kept where they stand and nothing pushed through the ground (run `chaos_zombie_r4i`, 0 findings). A sampled frame in the middle of the drop still shows the hips low but not yet down. The demon is flagged (2.5 times) but not settled, because its wing tips prop the body and lowering the hips would push them through the ground, which the ground pass undoes. Wings need their own handling.
Only two vanilla infantry deaths exist (GER_infantry death_mg and death_rifle, none in the DLC folders), so the calibration stays on them. A settle that leaves a hold says why in its record: a foot far beyond the hips' reach, or the skin outside the support resting too close to the ground.

## Tripo settings

These gave the best result for the price in the kit's own tests (an orc and a zombie, November 2026). They are a starting point, not a law.

| --- | --- | --- |
| Tool | Model, HD Model | Comes textured. Smart Mesh sculpts sharper detail but comes untextured, in loose parts, and costs more |
| AI model | H3.1 | Same price as H3.0 at these settings |
| Input | One 3D-ready picture | It was enough for a clean model. Generated side views did not improve the result |
| Ultra Mesh Quality | off | It adds 15 credits. The map camera does not show the difference |

| Topology | Triangle | HOI4 meshes are triangles |
| Polycount | 20,000 | The unit profile's working budget |

### By agent: how the agent drives Tripo

The agent works in your Chrome through Claude in Chrome, on the tab where you are signed in to Tripo Studio. It follows the same card as you would.

- **A promotion dialog is closed, never used.** Tripo may cover a page with a limited-time offer or another promotion. The agent closes it with the dialog's own close button (or Escape), does not claim the offer, and never presses a button inside it. If the dialog has no close button, the agent stops and says so.
- It opens pages by their address when it knows it: the image tool at `studio.tripo3d.ai/workspace/generate-image` and a model's Animate page at `studio.tripo3d.ai/workspace/animate/<asset id>` (see steps 1 and 5).
- It loads `.tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/tripo_studio.js` into the page. The helpers read the credit balance and the price on each button and refuse to press Generate when the price differs from the card. On the Animate page it types each preset's name into the search box (`TS.search`) and clicks the tile it finds (`TS.presetPoint`).
- It uploads the picture with the extension's file upload, never through a file dialog.
- To rig a model again on a fresh asset, it uploads the model's own export (**Upload 3D Model**, free). Tripo then shows **Find the Best Angle** with the model seen from its side. Confirm it unchanged: turning it there rotated the ZIN devil's mesh a quarter turn against the skeleton Tripo then built (2026-11-22).
- It presses each paid button once, then waits for the new task. A second press starts a second paid task.
- It never reloads the Tripo page in the middle of a paid step.
- Exports go to your Downloads folder. The agent moves them into the inbox and renames them. It takes only a file created after its own click (newer than a marker file it touches first), never "the newest file of that type", because Downloads also holds your own files and a blocked download leaves an old file the newest.
- Chrome lets one page start one download on its own. A second export from the same tab is blocked without a message, so the agent opens a fresh tab for each further download. A multi-view group's download button saves only its first view.
- Tripo's image tool saves its pictures as WebP with a `.png` name. The job's reference must be a real PNG, so the agent converts each downloaded picture before it uses it.
- It writes the balance before and after each paid step into the job's ledger, and stops at the budget.

This ran end to end on several models in November 2026. Tripo changes its page from time to time. When a helper stops finding a button, the agent says so and you finish that step by hand from the card.

## Old Meshy jobs

A job without `"provider": "tripo"` still runs the Meshy route: `run_pilot.py --phase candidate <unit>` makes the model through the Meshy API, and `run_pilot.py <unit>` rigs and animates it from Meshy's clip library. That route needs a Meshy key and a paid Meshy plan, and its prices are in `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/meshy_pricing.json`. `plan_clips.py` plans Meshy library clips so that two units do not share one. Exported Meshy units need nothing from Meshy any more: the checks, repairs, weapon fit and review all run on the files you have.

**An unarmed soldier-sized humanoid** (an undead soldier, a zombie, a civilian) takes `infantry_unarmed`, with the anatomy answers of any person (legs 2, arms 2, and no for the rest) and no `tripo.weapon` in the job. Do not take `infantry_melee` for it, whose clips are sword clips, and not `beast_biped`, which is for a large body (a yeti, a large ghoul) and is checked against the large-biped body preset that a soldier-sized model does not fit. The anatomy has no question about a held weapon, so an unarmed unit adds no key for it.

Every type gets the six land-unit roles: idle, move, attack, defend, retreat and death. A Tripo job picks one clip per role from the candidates it exported (see [Pick the clips by measurement](#pick-the-clips-by-measurement)), and the export's stay-in-place option keeps walks from sliding. The clips column above describes the motion each role should read as. For an old Meshy job most roles list two library candidates in `unit_types.json`, so the clip plan can give two units of one type different clips. A `vehicle` has `calibration_group: vehicle`: its checks use vanilla's tanks and trucks as the limit, not its soldiers.

Mounts are built to vanilla's horse size on the map, which is smaller than vanilla infantry. A mounted unit is two jobs, a `mount` and a `rider`. The mount's entity attaches the rider's entity to its `Saddle_Node`, the way vanilla cavalry does, so one horse can carry several kinds of rider.

The Tripo route's other rules apply as for a humanoid: grounding of every exported frame, the weight smoothing (with the `Load` bone locked) and the stretch checks. `tripo_checks.py` runs quadruped rules instead of the humanoid ones (`lib/quadruped_checks.py`): TRIPO-LOOP-POP, TRIPO-FOOT-BELOW-GROUND for the hooves, TRIPO-NOT-DOWN (the torso ends within 5% of the height of the ground), TRIPO-LOAD-BELOW-GROUND and TRIPO-LOAD-STRETCH (more than 0.5% of the load's edges stretch: a plank never bends), and these:

The seam is the skin the load blends into and every edge from the load to the body, and each edge belongs to the part its body end follows. A share of the whole mesh or of the whole seam misses a band: round 9's death stretched 0.5% of all edges against vanilla's 4.2% while the ear roots and the neck tore, and run 3's retreat creased the top of the neck against the tower's front while only 0.65% of the whole seam stretched. ### Tripo winged quadrupeds

A dragon, a griffin or any four-legged body with two membrane wings is a `winged_quadruped`. Export Tripo's plain textured model into the inbox as `<asset>__model.glb`, as for a mount. `measure_body.py` fits it to the `winged_quadruped` preset when the job's anatomy says it has wings. "unit_type": "winged_quadruped", "provider": "tripo", "tripo": {},
"anatomy": {"legs": 4, "arms": 0, "wings": true, "tail": true, ...},

**Changing a winged quadruped's pose, step by step.** The defaults above are the rig's own (`quadruped_layout.py`, `quadruped_wings.py`), so a new winged unit gets them without any change. When a clip must change:

1. Measure the last run's exported files first: `tripo/tripo_checks.py <unit folder> --jobs-config <jobs> --slug <slug> --json <out>`. Its `numbers` give each clip's `game_read`, `side_reach_over_idle`, `head_facing_turn`, `snout_screen_drop` and `deformed_share`.
2. Try the change offline: `tripo/pose_preview.py <unit folder> --landmarks <job root> --out <folder> --plan plan.json`, with the constant changed in the plan's `set`. Run the same checks on the folder it writes. A run is spent only when the checks read 0 findings there.
3. Write a turn of the head as where the head faces (`aim_head`, the Head entry of `STRIKE_NECK` and `GUARD_NECK_S`), never as the head bone's own turn: the neck's turns carry the head.
4. A pose that holds the wings back is `held_back(...)`: it keeps the fold's curl and spread and changes only where the fold points (sweep, rise, roll). Never unbend the fold or close the fan to make a pose narrower: the first spreads the wing out to the side, the second crushes the membrane.
5. A body that moves over planted feet uses `planted_crouch` (back for the defend, `forward=True` for the attack's lunge) within the hip limit TRIPO-LEG-ROOT-STRETCH allows.
6. When the change is to the rig itself (where a bone lies), try it on files made with the old rig: `tripo/pose_preview.py <unit folder> --landmarks <job root> --out <folder> --rerig --fit` moves the bones, weights their skin again by the rig's rule, writes the clips, fits the weights to them as the route does and writes the clips again on the fitted mesh. It saves the new landmarks as `<folder>/landmarks.json`: later plans on that folder use `--landmarks <folder>/landmarks.json`. Without `--fit` the raw weights fail checks the route would pass. The fit takes about two minutes.
7. Before the run, render the attack's blow, the defend's middle and the retreat's peak from the game camera beside the idle (`pose_preview.py ... --plan` with `times`, then `tripo/clip_sheet.py --views hoi4 front left`) and look at them.

### Check the body before you rig it

The body column names a body preset in `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/body_presets.json`. `measure_body.py` counts the feet on the ground and measures the torso and the reach of the lower body, then says which preset the model fits and how sure it is. It measures the body only, not separate plates, a quiver or a weapon. It fails a model with pieces floating apart from the body, so you drop them before paying for the rig. It passes when the job's unit type uses that preset. It fails when they disagree, for example a `beast_biped` job whose model came out on four legs, and names the unit types that fit. It escalates when no preset fits, for example a body with wings, extra arms or tentacles, which is rigged and animated live in Blender. It reads files only.

The anatomy you gave in step 1 tells the check what a measurement cannot see: `"anatomy": {"legs": 2, "arms": 2, "wings": false, "tail": false, "extra_limbs": false, "vehicle": false, "rider_on_mount": false}`. When you know better than the measurement, set `"body_preset"` in the job and say why in `"body_preset_reason"`.

## Automatic checks

Run them on a unit's folder at any time with the command in step 10. Without `--verdict` it lists every finding.

| Rule | Check | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| UNIT-BONES, UNIT-INFLUENCES | At most 50 bones, at most 4 bone weights per vertex | The game's shader skins no more (`gfx/FX/pdxmesh.shader`) |
| UNIT-TRIANGLES, UNIT-STREAM-VERTICES | Triangles within the profile's budget, at most 65,535 vertices per stream | Big meshes slow the map down |
| UNIT-ANIM-JOINTS | Every animation moves only bones the mesh has | Other bones are ignored and the unit freezes |
| UNIT-ANIM-FPS | Every clip of a soldier, rider or creature plays at 30 frames a second | Vanilla's do, and Paradox's own exporter knows only 15, 30 and 60. A Blender scene from factory settings runs at 24, so a clip keyed for 30 and written at 24 plays a quarter too slowly. The kit's exports retime to 30 and keep each clip's length in seconds |
| UNIT-ROLE-MISSING | Every role has an animation | A missing role shows a frozen pose |
| UNIT-NODE-MISSING, UNIT-NODE-ORIENTATION | A mount has a `Saddle_Node`, exported without rotation | The rider has nothing to sit on, and a turned node tips him over |
| UNIT-DDS-MIPS, UNIT-DDS-FORMAT | Textures are DXT5 or DXT1 with mip levels | Without mips the zoomed-out map shimmers. Uncompressed textures use four times the memory |
| UNIT-DDS-EMISSIVE | Normal maps have a black blue channel | The game reads blue in a normal map as glow |
| UNIT-DDS-SHINY | Specular maps stay about as dull as vanilla's | Shinier maps show as white speckles. The pipeline scales Meshy's metalness and gloss down to vanilla's range, and maps Tripo's gloss onto vanilla's |
| UNIT-HOLE | No more open edges, and no larger open loop, than vanilla units | The game hides back faces, so a gap shows the ground through the unit |
| UNIT-WINDING, UNIT-INSIDE-OUT | No triangles facing the wrong way, and no part turned inside out | The same culling makes them look like holes from one side |
| UNIT-NORMALS | No larger share of corner normals pointing against their faces than vanilla's worst | They shade as pale flat facets |
| UNIT-BRIDGE | No surface joining body parts that move apart, such as a hand fused to a thigh | Every clip tears the join into spikes and webs |
| UNIT-ANIM-STRETCH | No frame stretches or squashes the surface more than vanilla clips do | Catches weights leaking across body parts and torn joins in every frame |
| UNIT-ANIM-GROUND | Feet stay on the ground in every frame (a dead body may lie anywhere) | Floating and sinking units |
| UNIT-SEAM | No seam between the legs whose two sides follow one leg each, unblended | Parting the legs tears the seam open |
| UNIT-ANIM-KNEE | No knee bends backward in any frame | A leg solver can pick the wrong side, and the unit walks like a bird |
| UNIT-ANIM-STILL | Every clip moves at least as much as the stillest vanilla clip | A near-frozen clip reads as a static pose |
| UNIT-ANIM-SPIN | No joint turns farther in one frame than vanilla's fastest (37 degrees for soldiers, riders and creatures) | Keys on opposite sides of the quaternion sphere make Blender turn the long way, and a limb or polearm sweeps through the ground or the head in a frame or two |
| UNIT-LOOSE-PART | Every separate part (a strap, a plate, a quiver) is skinned and stays on the body in every frame, as close as vanilla's `GER_infantry` and `cavalry_horse` parts do. Any number of parts is fine. A held prop (a separate shape bound wholly to one bone) is one part however many pieces it has, judged against the hand that holds it | A part that floats off the body, or slides away when the unit moves, looks broken at every zoom |
| UNIT-PROP-CLIP (warning) | Names the frames in which a held prop goes inside the body, with the holding hand and forearm left out, in-betweens included | A polearm can swing through a head or a leg between two clear keys |
| UNIT-ANIM-SAME | No two roles of one unit play the same motion (a rider's idle, move and retreat may, since the mount's gait carries it) | Each role must read as itself |
| UNIT-ANIM-SHARED | With `--mod-root`: no clip is one another unit of the mod already plays, unless this unit is its reskin (`--reskin-of <unit>`) | Every unit has its own animations |
| UNIT-REG-* | With `--mod-root`: the mesh, every animation, an entity with every state and a sub-unit sprite are registered | Unregistered files never show |

The surface and motion limits are the worst values measured on the game's own units of the same group: `organic` for soldiers, riders, mounts and animals, `vehicle` for tanks, guns and trucks, `air_naval` for planes and ships. They live in `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/deformation_calibration.json`. After a game update, measure them again with `calibrate_deformation.py "<game folder>" --out .tools/3d_pipeline/config/deformation_calibration.json`. Never raise a limit by hand to pass a unit: fix the unit.

`--verdict <file>` gives the short verdict instead of the full list. It escalates a rule no script fixes, such as a knee that bends backward or a clip another unit plays. `--repair-textures` first rewrites uncompressed or unmipped textures as DXT5 with mips. When a mount and its rider share one folder, check each with `--stem`, for example `--stem <unit> --unit-type mount` and `--stem <unit>_rider --unit-type rider`.

### Holes the player sees

A missing face, a face turned the wrong way, or a one-sided sheet seen from behind shows the ground through the unit. An open-edge count cannot tell those from the open edges a model keeps on trims, plates and straps that never show, so the rigged unit profiles judge holes by looking (`"holes": "silhouette"`). The model check in step 3 only records open edges. `check_holes.py` takes a unit folder or a provider `.glb` (steps 3 and 10).

It renders the unit in Blender from 17 views (eight around it, low and high, and the map camera), at rest and at several frames of every clip, once with back faces hidden and once with them shown. Pixels that only back faces cover are holes. Vanilla shows a few specks too, such as a sleeve seen edge-on, so the unit passes when its largest patch is no larger than the largest `GER_infantry` and `cavalry_horse` show the same way: 58 pixels in a death clip and 50 in every other clip, at the 256-pixel tile size. Death clips have their own limit, because a body lying down shows larger specks from low views. The 50 comes from the horse's one-sided reins. A `.glb` is checked at rest, before the rig.

`sheets/holes_worst.png` shows the worst views, the unit grey and the holes red. A failing verdict names the free `repair_unit.py --repairs winding holes`. The limits live in `.tools/3d_pipeline/config/silhouette_calibration.json`. After a game update, measure them again with `calibrate_holes.py "<game folder>" --work <scratch folder>`, which takes about half an hour.

### Put the weapon in the hand

Soldiers, riders, giants and other armed units hold their weapon, which is generated as a separate model. A Tripo unit gets it from `tripo/arm_unit.py` (see [Armed units](#armed-units)). For an old Meshy job, `fit_prop.py` puts it in the hand. Name `--bone` by the rig's side, as `tripo.weapon.hand` is:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/fit_prop.py <unit folder> --prop <weapon job>/provider/downloads/generation_model.glb --bone RightHand --length 0.55 --grip 0.15 --out <job folder>/props
```

It scales the weapon to `--length` of the unit's height and puts its grip (`--grip` of its length from the butt) in the palm. A smart-topology prop that arrives in many small pieces keeps them: pieces within 3 % of the prop's size of each other chain into one prop, and only lone fragments are dropped. It tries 42 directions over every clip and keeps the one whose far end least often passes through the body or into the ground. Then it binds the weapon to the hand bone and writes the unit with the weapon's three textures to `<job folder>/props/files`. The clips are not touched. Running it again on the same folder replaces the weapon it fitted before.

The verdict passes when the weapon never goes below the ground and passes through the body in at most 2 % of its sampled points. It also writes `<job folder>/props/registration_prop.md`, the `meshsettings` block to paste into the unit's `pdxmesh`. If no direction clears the clips, try a shorter `--length` (0.4 clears most heads and hips), then another `--grip` between 0.1 and 0.3, or `--butt low`. A weapon held in both hands always needs live work for the second hand.

### Independent review

Checks count things. They cannot tell you that a cape tears into spikes in the death clip, or that a sword passes through a leg. Look at every clip from every side, and let someone else look, because the person who made a unit sees what they meant to make.

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/review_unit.py <unit folder> --mod-root . --out <job folder>/evidence/review
```

It renders each clip from front, left, back, right, the map camera and below. Every round shows the same frames: one every half second of each clip and its last (`--step`), so two rounds can be compared. On top of those come the frames a check flagged (`--findings <tripo_checks.json>`) and the frames where the surface stretches most and where the unit sits lowest and highest, each captioned with why it is there. These extremes move when a clip changes a little (the orc's lowest attack frame moved from 18 to 38 between two rounds), so they are never shown in place of the fixed frames. A long clip's sheet is split into pages of six frames. Back faces are hidden as in the game on a magenta background, so magenta inside the unit is a hole, and a dark disc marks the ground. Each clip also gets a diagnostics sheet in flat colours: yellow to red where the surface stretches or squashes, cyan at an open edge, blue below the ground. It writes `REVIEW_REQUEST.md` with the reviewer instructions and the quality bar. Keep `--out` in the job folder, never under `gfx/`, or the renders ship with the mod.

Give `REVIEW_REQUEST.md` and the sheets, and nothing else, to a reviewer that has not seen how the unit was made: a new agent session, a subagent or a friend. Do not tell it what you fixed or what you think is fine. Save its whole reply and record it:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/review_unit.py <unit folder> --out <job folder>/evidence/review --record reply.md
```

The unit passes only on `VERDICT: pass` with `DEFECTS: none`. Any listed defect fails it. After a fail, fix the cause, export again and ask a new reviewer. Keep every reply as the record that the unit was looked at.

### Review rounds

The bar stays at zero findings (decision 3D-7). Rounds converge only when every reviewer gets the same request, and the camera angles never change between rounds:

- **Fixed notes.** If part of the unit's look could pass for a defect, such as a torn hem or torn wing membranes, say so once in a notes file in the job folder and pass it every round with `--note-file <job folder>/review_notes.md` (or `--note "..."`). The reviewer still judges everything the note does not name, including any hole in the body.
- **The checklist.** The reviewer goes through the quality bar item by item and ends with a `CHECKLIST:` block. A reply that skips an item is recorded as a fail.
- **Changed clips, then a full round.** After you fix some clips, `--clips attack death` renders only those and the rest pose, and the reviewer judges only them. A unit is accepted only after a full round, without `--clips`, by a new reviewer. When an intermediate round passes, the verdict names that full round.
- **Close-ups.** A reviewer can misread a small dark feature seen side-on, such as a large black eye read as an open mouth. A closed mesh with no winding conflict has no hole there. Name the feature in the notes and render with `--size 768`.

## When the reviewer finds defects

The checks pass anything within vanilla's limits, so a reviewer can still see defects:

| The reviewer sees | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| A hole (magenta, red on `holes_worst.png`, or a cyan rim on the diagnostics sheet) | `repair_unit.py ... --repairs winding holes --max-hole-edges 32`, then check the copy. Holes left after that need faces rebuilt in Blender, or a new model |
| Surface that tears or smears where two body parts meet, red on the diagnostics sheet in every clip | `repair_unit.py ... --repairs bridges weights` |
| Skin that swells or stretches at a joint in the clips, red on the diagnostics sheet around armpits, waist or hips | `repair_unit.py ... --repairs stretch` (add `--locked-bones <weapon bone>` for a held weapon) |
| A foot above or below the ground | `repair_unit.py ... --repairs ground` |
| The weapon through the head or body | `fit_prop.py` again on the same copy, first with a shorter `--length` such as 0.4, then another `--grip` between 0.1 and 0.3. A two-handed hold is made live |
| A part that tears or crushes only in some clips (a death or an attack that twists the torso hard), or a clip that does not read as its role | No script fixes the motion itself. Find another preset or tool for those clips, or ask before paying for others. Keying them by hand in Blender is the last resort, with the reason recorded |

Fix the free ones first, in this order: the mesh repairs, then the weapon. Render again and ask a new reviewer, then take what is left to Blender.

### Small fixes and last resort: live Blender

When a small fix is needed, or no rig tool or preset works for a unit (wings, extra arms, a tail, a vehicle, a clip that failed review twice), your agent may pose the unit in a live Blender session through the Blender Lab MCP, one key at a time, and look at every key before it keeps it. It records why a tool or preset could not do the job. It costs nothing.

The kit's helpers in `.tools/3d_pipeline/authoring/live_helpers.py` turn, place, measure, render and key only what the agent names. They solve a leg with the knee toward the toes, put a hand on a point with a temporary IK, plant a foot, spread a lean down the spine, weight a crotch seam, keep a hand on a weapon wherever it moves, and render the reviewer's views. `export_clip` writes a clip over the action's own frame range at 30 frames a second. `normalize_quaternion_keys` flips keys that would make a joint turn the long way. `stretch_share` gives the share of edges the stretch check counts at a frame. `lowest_regions` lists the lowest point of each body region and prop, to find what holds a dead body up. `clear_sharp_faces` removes import flags that push a stream past 65,535 vertices.

The agent's guide is the skill's [live authoring reference](../.claude/skills/hoi4-3d-model-pipeline/references/live-authoring.md), including vehicles (wheel pivots, fused rigid parts, turret traverse). The result goes through the same checks and the same review as any other unit.

## Static and rigid-part models

Buildings, landmarks, ships, tanks and planes have their own route. Tripo makes a plain textured model (no rig and no clips, 30 credits: HD Model, PBR on, 20,000 triangles, Generate in Parts off, or on for a subject with small detailed pieces, see **Parts** below). One free script turns it into HOI4 files, and a second renders the review sheets:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/static_model.py <inbox>/<name>.json --out <job folder>
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/tripo/static_sheets.py <job folder>/mod/gfx/models/<folder>/<name> --model-kind <kind> --out <job folder>/review
```

**Check a Smart Mesh trial before you pay for its texture.** Smart Mesh makes untextured variants first, and its Texture button costs 20. Free first:

1. On the Model page, Smart Mesh, open **Topology**, pick Triangle and turn off **Use Same Polygon Count**. Give the four variants four counts up to the kind's budget (a building: 6,000, 8,000, 10,000 and 12,000), so one paid texture can go to the count that came out cleanest.
2. Export each untextured variant (free, GLB), each from its own browser tab.
3. Run the route on each with `--geometry-only`. It packs flat grey maps and adds a flat UV layer, runs every geometry step and check, writes `GEOMETRY_ONLY.txt` beside the files and prints `geometry pass` or `geometry fail`. Nothing from such a run is shipped.
4. Texture only the variant that passes and looks cleanest on its sheets, then run the route again without the flag.

Generate Multi-Views is free, but for a building seen from three quarters it invented parts the picture does not show (a dormer, a fourth chimney, a moved crane). Look at each view before you give it to a model, and use a single picture when the views disagree.

The job file holds `name` and `model_kind`: `building`, `landmark`, `ship`, `vehicle` (a tank) or `plane`. The GLB is `<name>.glb` next to it (or the job's `glb`). A GLB made in parts is reduced part by part unless the job sets `"parts": false`, and `part_shape_iou` (default 0.97, as in the unit route) sets how closely each reduced part must keep its silhouettes. Add `--plan` to see what the stage would do, without Blender. The script writes a small mod tree under `<job folder>/mod`: the `.mesh`, one `.anim` per role and the three DXT5 textures under `gfx/models/<folder>/<name>/`, and the registration in three files, as vanilla lays it out: `gfx/entities/<name>.gfx` with the `pdxmesh` inside `objectTypes`, `gfx/models/<folder>/<name>/<name>.asset` with the `animation` blocks (file names relative to that folder, which is how the game reads them), and `gfx/entities/<name>.asset` with the entity alone. HOI4 1.19.4 refused a single `.asset` holding all three ("Unexpected token: objectTypes"), so the model never showed. The folder also gets `runtime/registration.md` and `runtime/files/`, the unit route's layout, so `wire_unit.py <job folder> --mod-root <mod>` wires the model into a mod. Then it runs the checks below and prints a verdict.

Every number comes from vanilla files. `calibrate_static_kinds.py` measures them and writes `config/static_kinds.json`, each with the files it came from. Run it again after a game update. The role clips are read from the installed game when a model is made.

| `building` | the area the map camera sees of vanilla's four facilities, entity scale 0.6 | one static shape, no bones, a `Collision` box, the `lamp` locator | none |

What the stage does, in order:

- **Yard (buildings).** Tripo often stands a map building on a wide raised earth or paved yard, which sits on the map like a tile under it. Before the model is sized, the route finds the yard (a piece within 12 % of the height that covers a quarter of the plan) and pulls it in toward the building along rays from its centre, so it reaches at most 6 % of the building's own size past the building, its ragged outline kept, and lowers its rim to the ground over the outer quarter of that reach. Everything that is not the yard or a small loose piece holds the yard under it. Small loose pieces lying on it (barrels, heaps) move in with it and keep their shape. Faces and UVs stay. `yard` in `blender_report.json` gives the reach and rim height before and after. A forge works' Smart Mesh yard reached 28 % past the building with its rim 2.6 % of the height up, and it now reaches 6 % with its rim at 0.5 %.
- **Facing.** The model is turned so its front faces -y, as vanilla's do. A tank's front is where its turret reaches further (the barrel), a ship's bow is its narrower end, a plane's nose is the end nearer the wing. Set `front` in the job file when a rule gets it wrong.
- **Size and place.** Scaled to the kind's vanilla size at the reference entity's scale, centred and grounded where vanilla's are. A ship's waterline is where its hull reaches 95 % of its beam, as on vanilla hulls. A tank's height and footprint (the square root of its length times its width) are bounded by vanilla's reference tank, the one the game-size sheet draws beside it (`size.span_bound`, ITA_tank_light): each may be at most 5 % over that tank's share of the main measure. A taller or broader model is sized on that span instead, and STATIC-SIZE measures the same way. A Smart Mesh tank sized on the area the camera sees stood 28 % taller than ITA_tank_light. It is now 5 % taller, with a footprint 21 % smaller, because its own proportions are taller than vanilla's.
- **Triangles and textures.** Faces turned against their neighbours are turned back, piece by piece (a piece keeps Blender's recalculated winding only when it leaves fewer conflicts, and then each face at odds with two or more neighbours is turned while that helps), doubled faces are dropped and closed pieces facing inward are turned out, once after the weld and again after the holes are closed. Needle slivers that stick out of the surface (a triangle over 20 times as long as it is high, with an open long edge, its tip at least half its length off every other triangle) and tiny loose open flakes (pieces at most 5 % of the model's size, at most 1 % thick, with at least half their edges open) are deleted first. A closed thin part such as a barrel or an antenna has no open edge, so it is never touched. A Smart Mesh tank's six fender gussets, open wedges that reviewers saw as stray spikes along the hull, go this way. Small open loops (up to 32 edges) are closed, as `repair_unit.py --repairs holes` does, in one flat colour so the texture does not swirl across them. A building's or landmark's open rim on the ground (a paved disc with no underside) is closed as well. Then each flat one-sided card (an open piece thinner than 2 % of its own largest side and at least 5 % of the model, such as a drive sprocket or a bogie arm modelled as one sheet) is solidified, centred on the sheet: 1 % of the model's size thick, at most a quarter of its own smallest side, and at most 0.8 of the gap to any piece in front of or behind it on each side, so it touches nothing new. A piece that already passes through the card or rests against it does not bound it. The rim faces take one colour, and the triangles the cards need are kept back from the reduction's target. The reduction after the cuts leaves the thickened cards alone. Then the model is reduced to the triangle budget and texture size of the most detailed vanilla model of the kind: faces that lie flat together are merged first, then the rest is collapsed. The silhouettes are compared before and after the reduction, before the holes are closed, and a reduction that changes the outline fails the stage (STATIC-SHAPE). A cap over an opening adds to the outline by design. The maps are packed for `PdxMeshAdvanced`: the normal map as RRxG, the gloss remapped onto the distribution of the kind's vanilla specular maps, the specular level vanilla's.
- **Parts.** Tripo's Generate in Parts exports one mesh node a part (a hull, a turret, each track band, a lion, a chimney). The stage keeps each part's faces apart (`lib/static_parts.py`): each part is welded on its own, so parts that only touch stay closed and separate, and when the model is over its target each part is reduced on its own, flat faces merged first and then collapsed, to a share of the target. The shares follow each part's need, half by its surface area and half by its bend (each edge's length times the angle between its two faces, so a bogie or a mane counts more than a flat plate). A part never gets more than it has, nor less than 24 triangles (or all of a smaller part). Every reduced part is checked as the unit route checks a reduced weapon (`part_shape_iou`: no new shells, the front, side and top silhouettes of the part on its own frame), and it may not have more open edges than it had. Each part's check is its own, so the stage searches each part's least passing share on its own (down by halves from its share by need while it passes, up by doubling while it fails, then by bisection to 5 %). When the least shares fit the target, the rest is shared by need on top of them. When they do not, the stage stops with STATIC-PARTS, which names each part that needs more than its share by need, what it needs and its first problem. Never simplify the model to fit: ask the user whether to accept a lower `part_shape_iou` or a larger budget. For a tank, the job field `running_gear_triangles` raises the budget for its tracks, bogies and wheels (`static_kinds.with_running_gear`). The model then has more triangles than any vanilla tank, so every check warns STATIC-TRIANGLES-RAISED until the user accepts it. The HD tank split into five parts (a stand-in for a parts export) needed 6,858 triangles at 0.97 against a light tank's target of 3,711: its two track bands with their bogies 3,355 and 2,395, the turret 376, the gun 78, the hull under its share of 673. The search takes about half an hour on that tank, most of it drawing silhouettes. Measured on the same tank against the HD source's own triangles, region by region, the whole-model reduction to 3,948 left the gun 12 triangles with silhouettes of 0.57 to 0.80, the turret 0.91 to 0.94 and the track bands 0.90 to 0.94. A trial in parts at `part_shape_iou` 0.94 (a trial, not a pass) kept the gun at 61 triangles and 0.85 to 0.92, the turret at 0.93 to 0.94 and the bands at 0.92 to 0.94, but the hull fell from 598 to 488 triangles: at a light tank's budget the parts that need more take it from the hull. Both runs passed the hole check (no hole pixel) and both had crumpled bogies. A silhouette in a part's own frame misses detail inside the outline (the hull passed at 24 triangles at 0.94), so look at the sheets as well as the numbers. The whole model is then judged by its silhouettes alone (STATIC-SHAPE), because parts that only touched come apart in their own reductions. `--plan` lists each part's triangles, need and first share. `reduction.parts` in `blender_report.json` holds each part's triangles before and after, its share, need, silhouette agreement, open edges and attempts. A building's or landmark's parts do not move and are joined into one static shape after their reductions. When the parts carry their own maps, the stage first writes one atlas (`lib/glb_atlas.py`, the receipt is `atlas` in `blender_report.json`). Each material's colour, normal and metallic-roughness maps go into a square tile sized by that material's share of the surface area, rounded down to a power of two, with an 8 pixel gutter of repeated edge pixels. The material factors are baked into the tiles and the UVs move into each tile. UVs outside 0 to 1, a primitive without UVs or a material, or a map that cannot be read stop the stage with STATIC-ATLAS. After the reduction, every vertex the collapse left more than 0.25 % of the model's size off its own part's source surface is moved back onto it (`tripo/static_mesh_ops.snap_to_source`, the receipt is `snap` in `blender_report.json`). Its corners take the source's UV there unless that is across a seam. No triangle is added or removed. On the parts tank at 7,473 triangles it moved 166 vertices and the largest distance fell from 0.73 % to 0.25 %, but the close-up did not change: the crumpled bogies and the spikes on the turret roof are too few triangles for the source's round spoked wheels and roof cupola, not stray vertices. A budget too small for the running gear's shape cannot be repaired after the reduction. Ask the user for a larger `running_gear_triangles` or for a running gear rebuilt from clean wheels.
- **Delighting.** Tripo's texture model can paint a cast shadow into the colour map (a hard dark band across a roof where a chimney "casts" one), fixed in a direction the game's light will contradict. The route lifts it (`lib/static_delight.py`). It draws each facet group (triangles that share edges and face one way) into texture space with the model's UVs and looks in each group for a region much darker than the group's median (under 0.65 of it), long (it runs across the group, 40 % of the group's longest extent), large (4 % of the group), sharp-edged and of the group's own colour family. A window, a door, a weathering streak, a stain or the soft darkening in a corner fails one of those tests and stays. The lift divides the shadow's low-frequency brightness out per channel and puts the surrounding brightness in, so the bricks and slates inside it stay. Every limit is a share of the group or a ratio of its median. The report says what was lifted (`delight` in `blender_report.json`). Before blaming the texture, look at it: a dark band on a roof in a review render may be the render's own sun shadow. The static review sheets render without cast shadows (`sun_shadow: false`), so a part never darkens another on them. On a tank's sheets made before that, reviewers read the barrel's shadow as a rod lying across the hull front that turned with the turret, and the turret's shadow as a crescent painted on the rear deck. Neither was in the model: the deck's texture had no crescent, and no turret edge passed through the hull in any frame. When a finding looks like a shadow, check that the sheets' `render_request.json` has `"sun_shadow": false` before changing the model.
- **Ring seam and contrast (tanks).** Tripo paints the sunk turret ring's band, which the turret hides, in whatever colour it likes: a bright yellow-green halo round the turret on the hull roof. The route finds the ring (its radius is the closest twentieth of the distances at which the model's edges cross the turret's cut, from the turret's axis) and the band of triangles from 0.85 to 1.5 of that radius sideways and from 0.3 of the turret's height under the cut to a tenth over it (by each triangle's centre, and by the 3D point under each texel: a big roof plate has its centre far out but its edge along the ring, and its texels near the ring belong to the band, `delight.ring_seam.band_texels_from_points`). The roof is the same heights out to 2.6 of the radius. A band texel more than 12 % brighter or darker than the roof's median, or in another colour family, takes the roof's median colour, brightened or darkened by its own deviation (at most 9 % down and 6 % up), so grain and rivets stay. A band no wilder than the roof round it is left alone. The report (`delight.ring_seam`) holds the share of band texels off the roof's colour before and after. Tripo's colour maps are also flatter than vanilla's: a light tank's texture spreads its brightness (standard deviation over mean, over the texels its faces use) by 0.25 to 0.64, a Smart Mesh tank's by 0.19, so at map size it is a flat slab. The route measures the nine vanilla light tanks' textures and lifts a flatter one to their lower quartile: it stretches the brightness about its median by that share of the measured spread (at most 1.5 times, colour kept), then darkens the model's foot and lightens its top by up to 15 %, as vanilla's light falls on a tank. The report (`delight.contrast`) holds the spread before and after.
- **Fittings, turret traverse and tread (tanks).** The step that turns a crumpled small fitting into its convex hull runs only when the model was reduced: a Smart Mesh tank under its target keeps its fittings as made (the M2A4 tank, 6,257 triangles against a target of 7,095, had 35 clean vision flaps and hinges turned into blocks and 29 edges between opposite faces, UNIT-WINDING). The turret turns only as far as it clears the hull: `static_kinds.turret_clear_yaw` turns the turret with its gun about its pivot in 2 degree steps each way, up to half a turn, until an edge of it and the hull cross as STATIC-PART-CLIP counts them, and each clip but the death keeps its turret turns' shape and timing, scaled toward rest on the side that went past the clear angle (`scaled_turns`). On the M2A4 the anti-aircraft post's foot swept through a deck fitting behind the turret in vanilla's idle (14 edges). The report holds `parts.turret_clear_yaw` and each clip's `turret_traverse`. The tread is drawn at a mean luma between 70 % of vanilla's light tanks' treads and their 96.5 (`tread_target_luma`, `track_tone` in the report): a run painted near black, as the M2A4's at 24, keeps its tint and is lifted so its plates, grousers and gaps read apart at close up and at game size.
- **Size reference (tanks).** A job may name the vanilla model of its kind it stands beside (`size_reference`, a path from `size.per_model` in `config/static_kinds.json` or its file stem, such as `USA_tank_light`). The route then sizes the model to that model's runtime measure, STATIC-SIZE checks against it (`check_unit_files.py --size-reference`) and the game-size sheet draws it beside the model (`static_sheets.py --size-reference`). Without it the model is sized to the kind's median, the Italian light tank. Vanilla's light tanks range from 2.13 to 2.94 at runtime: sized to the median, a US M2A4 read as two thirds to four fifths of the Italian tank to two reviewers, while it was 104 % of vanilla's US light tank. A vehicle named so is sized by its footprint (the square root of its length times its width) to the named model's (`size.per_model[].footprint`, from `calibrate_static_kinds.py`), and its height follows its own proportions, without the median reference's height and footprint bound (D-134). The map camera looks down, so the ground a vehicle covers reads as its size: sized by the camera area, the tall M2A4 covered 82 % of the US tank's footprint and four reviewers saw it 15 to 25 % smaller, while the real M2A4 and M3 cover about the same ground. The tank preset asks for it.
- **Collar, open sheets, metal (tanks).** The small faces that stand a hair out of the roof round the turret's base (sloping more than 30 degrees, within 0.04 of the turret's height tall, in 0.85 to 1.15 of the ring radius) are snapped down into the roof plane before the cuts. A hull sheet left open along an edge over 3 % of the model long (a track's inner wall, a fender plate) gets a reversed copy of its faces within 15 degrees of the open edge's face, so it shows from both sides. A tank's specular map takes the metallic value of the reference tanks' maps (129 for the light tanks) when Tripo's carries none. The report holds the counts (`parts.collar`, `parts.double_sided_sheets`, `delight.metallic`). A thin bright arc round the turret base was still visible after these. It was texture paint after all: faces coloured by index showed the arc on two big roof triangles (UV chart 0.07 by 0.15, a 2 m plate) whose centres lie outside the band, so the ring seam skipped them, and 12 % of their texels were bright (up to luma 220 against the roof's 103). Matching the band by each texel's own 3D point removed it (hoi4 rest tile, both close-ups, below and back views clean).
- **Round after the arc (tanks).** Three more rules, found on a Smart Mesh light tank. (1) The map-size footprint: the size rule bounded the height to vanilla's reference plus 5 % (`size.span_bound`), which held a tall Tripo tank to 80 % of vanilla's footprint (a flat olive slab at map size). The measure is now the mean of the one at which the height equals vanilla's and the one at which the footprint does, so the height is over vanilla's by as much as the footprint is under it (13 to 14 % each on that tank, from 5 % and 20 %). It only relaxes the height bound, never past the footprint bound or what the camera sees (`sk.main_measure`). (2) The crumpled blob: small separate closed pieces (bounding box diagonal at most 10 % of the model's) whose volume is not within 0.9 to 1.05 of their convex hull's become that hull, in the colour of the nearest original face (`static_mesh_ops.hull_crumpled_pieces`, report `crumpled_pieces`: 10 pieces on that tank, among them the three lumps under the rear deck). (3) The pale lower rear panel: its cause was a wall facing into the hull. Its two big triangles had their normals pointing forward, so from behind they were culled and the camera saw the hull's floor (up-facing, lit like a roof) through them. A wall whose normal ray meets the model and whose reverse ray escapes (`sk.inside_out_walls`, 16 triangles) gets a reversed copy (`inside_out_walls` in the report). A texture darkening of the floor was tried first and removed: STATIC-SHADOW read it as a painted shadow. Track tone: vanilla's light tanks' tread textures have a mean luma of 96.5 (`rules.tracks.tone`, from `calibrate_static_kinds.py`), and the route darkens a run painted brighter than that to it. That tank's tread texture was already at luma 36, darker than vanilla's, so the olive look at map size comes from the wide fenders over the tracks, not the track colour. Round 12 (tank_sm5k): a piece whose hull fills under 0.6 of its oriented bounding box (a wedge), or under 0.75 when it is thinner than 0.2 of its longest side (a plate), is replaced by that box instead of its hull (`HULL_BOX_FILL`, `HULL_PLATE_FLAT`, `HULL_PLATE_FILL`), because the hull of a box a reduction cut into a prism is a thin slanted wedge (the two rear blocks under the deck, the turret's top plate). Round 13 (tank_sm5l) corrected round 12, whose run (tank_sm5k) was worse than the hull-only one (tank_sm5h): the pinched-piece rule is dropped (its 6 boxes of already convex pieces stuck out of the glacis and front plates as shards, and a wedge box under the lower front hull), a box replaces a piece only when its volume is at most `HULL_BOX_GROWTH` (1.5) times the piece's own (else the hull stands in), and a piece that is moving gear (`sk.running_gear`: wheels, sprockets, idlers, track runs) is never boxed (its hull may stand in: a lump welded to a wheel bracket passes for gear by position, and leaving it crumpled fails UNIT-INSIDE-OUT). The report lists `box`, the volumes, the centre and the extent of each. Not solved: the creased lump on the turret's rear wall is turret shell (a protrusion about 0.9 by 0.4 by 0.35 at the turret's rear, centred on the axis at about 3.4 up), not a separate piece, so none of these rules reach it. It needs a rule on fittings attached to a part.
- **Shading.** Tripo's GLB brings one smooth normal a vertex, averaged across hard corners, and a glTF import keeps it as custom normals. The route used to carry those into the `.mesh`, and the weld averaged them further: 75 to 82 % of a Smart Mesh tank's, factory's and landmark's surface had a corner whose normal leaned more than 45 degrees off its own face. A plain grey face then showed light diagonal streaks and an X-like smear (a factory's lean-to annex), and a flat cap read as a cone (a tank's empty turret ring). The route drops the imported normals and shades by its own rule (`static_mesh_ops.crease_shading`): smooth only across faces within 40 degrees of each other, sharp at an edge with one face or more than two, or a face with no area, and any face that still has a corner leaning past 40 degrees (a fan round a pointed tip) is shaded flat. `shading` in `blender_report.json` says what it found. STATIC-SHADING checks the exported normals.
- **Base slab.** A building or landmark often stands on a thin slab of ground. The route finds it (`lib/static_slab.py`): up-facing triangles in the lowest 12 % of the height that make one rectangle in the building's own frame, thin, reaching past the walls by 3 % of its width. It flattens the top to one plane, sets the outline to a rectangle by robust bounds of its edge vertices (a corner pulled out is pulled back) and gives the slab's faces flat shading normals, with sharp edges, because a slab corner shared by its top and sides otherwise averages the three faces. UVs and vertex count stay. A round base, a stepped base (a second tier of 25 % of the slab's area within three thicknesses above it, as a landmark's stairs) and a model with no slab are left alone (`slab` in `blender_report.json` says which).
- **Rigid parts.** A tank's turret starts above the largest step in the plan area of its height slices, where the slices above are turret-sized. On vanilla's own light tanks this rule puts 92 to 100 % of the surface where their skinning does. The barrel is the thin run in front of the turret. A plane's propeller is the blade disc at the nose. A tank modelled in pieces (Tripo's Smart Mesh) also gives up its road wheels (round pieces inside the track loop, their vertices about one distance from the axle and their rim going round, as far as vanilla's wheels) and its track runs (pieces half the hull long whose side view fills at most half its box, as a loop does: Smart Mesh runs fill 0.13 to 0.21 and every vanilla light tank's runs pass, while a parts export's track band with its bogies and fender fills 0.76 and stays on the hull, so its wheels do not take the scrolling tread): on vanilla's light tanks this rule finds every vertex their wheel bones carry. A wheel must also look the same turned half a turn, as vanilla's do, unless its rim closes a full circle (a toothed sprocket). A bogie arm or a coarse wheel that would turn like a spinning diamond stays on the hull. A plane's propeller ends at the cowling, the first slice behind the blade disc as narrow as the stretch behind it. In a model made in parts, a part with at least 90 % of its area on one side of the turret cut (or, over the cut, of the barrel cut) goes whole to that side and is not sliced: a turret part sunk a little into the hull stays one closed turret, and a hull part stays on the hull. A part that straddles a cut is cut as before. `parts.source_parts` in the report says which rigid part each source part ended on, and `parts.whole_parts` which went whole. Each part is split off with its own vertices, and each cut is closed with a cap in the colours around it, so a turning turret shows no hole. The caps on a tank's turret cut, the ring on the hull and the turret's underside, are shaded flat and take one dark texel: the texel deepest inside the largest patch of the darkest twentieth of the texels the model's faces cover, so the colour holds in the small mip levels (`lib/static_delight.hole_uv`, `hole` in `blender_report.json`). A ring capped in the colours around it and shaded smooth with the hull read as a neat cone-shaped lid when the death blew the turret off. Now it reads as the dark inside of an open ring. A piece the turret cut only clipped (lying wholly within 5 % of the turret's height above the cut, such as a fender corner or a bolt) stays on the hull, so it does not fly with the turret. Hull roof the cut only clipped stays on the hull: a Smart Mesh's roof plates and bolts lie a hair above the cut, so a piece above it that lies wholly within a tenth of the turret's height of the cut and wholly outside the ring (the radius above) is the hull's (`sk.hull_level_pieces`). Carried off with the turret it left a hole in the roof behind the ring and hung in the air beside the blown-off turret.
- **Scrolling tracks.** A tank's track runs scroll as vanilla's do. They get a second material with vanilla's `PdxMeshAdvancedAnim` shader and a tread texture of their own (`<name>_tracks_*.dds`): vanilla's tread size (64 by 32) and number of links a tile (5), in the colour Tripo painted the runs. Their UVs run across the run (0.03 to 0.97, as vanilla's) and round the loop each run makes, seen from the side, at the density vanilla's light tank has on its ground run (6.05 tile lengths a main measure), rounded to a whole number of tiles a loop so the seam does not jump. With v along the hull, the near-upright faces at a loop's curved ends got one stretched row of the tread, and a reviewer read them as see-through. The `.gfx` lists that second stream as `index = 1` of the shape, and vanilla's entity carries `texture_anim_speed = 0.5`, which scrolls it. The review sheets scroll the tread in the move clip at that speed.
- **Clips.** Each role plays vanilla's clip of the kind, moved onto the model's own pivots and scaled to its size: the barrel recoils by the share of its own length vanilla's does. A tank's turret also turns 21.7 degrees (vanilla's idle traverse) before the shot and back after it. A propeller turns 36 degrees a frame over whole turns, so the loop does not jump, and the plane is lifted so the spinning blades clear the ground. Road wheels turn by vanilla's road-wheel spin, faster for a smaller wheel so the rim moves as far. A tank's tracks and wheels stay on the ground in every clip but its death. Vanilla's light tank moves bounce the whole tank, tracks included, through `tank_base` by 7 to 10 % of its height round the ground. The ground rule lifted the dips and kept the rises, and reviewers saw the tank hop for half the cycle. Each frame the running gear now keeps only its turn about z and its horizontal place, its lowest point on the ground, and the hull keeps vanilla's own bob on its suspension (`chassie` against `tank_base`). No frame sinks below the ground (a death keeps vanilla's settling), and a turret blown off in a death turns onto its flat base, drops to the ground and lands clear of the hull and its running gear, moved the short way off it with a clearance of a tenth of its own size. A cut part is set off its cut by a hair, so the file checks do not weld its rim to the body's.
- **Mod text.** The entity is vanilla's entity for the kind, renamed. An event at a node the model lacks is dropped, and a state playing an animation the model lacks plays `idle`.

The checks are `check_unit_files.py <folder> --model-kind <kind>`, the unit rules with the kind's roles and its group's limits (`vehicle` for tanks, buildings and landmarks, `air_naval` for ships and planes), plus these:

| Rule | Check |
| --- | --- |
| STATIC-SIZE, STATIC-GROUND | The size and the ground (a ship's waterline and draft) are vanilla's |
| STATIC-TRIANGLES, STATIC-SHADER | Within the kind's budget, with its shader (a tank's track runs may use `PdxMeshAdvancedAnim`) |
| STATIC-BONES, STATIC-LOCATORS, STATIC-COLLISION | The bones, locators and 8-vertex `Collision` box vanilla's entity and clips use |
| STATIC-RIGID | Every rendered vertex follows one bone wholly, so no part bends |
| STATIC-PART-GAP | No part lifts off the surface of the part it is mounted on in any frame, deaths excepted. The distance is to points spread over the mount's triangles, because a reduced hull top can be a few large triangles with no vertex under a turned turret |
| STATIC-PART-CLIP | No part mounted on the body (a turret, with the gun on it) passes into the body in any frame of any clip, deaths included: no edge of either goes through the other's surface (24 frames a clip). Edges that already cross in the rest pose, and edges in the seam band within 5 % of the turret's height of its cut, are left out: a Smart Mesh turret ring is a faceted cylinder sunk into the hull roof, and as it turns its facets cross the roof's edges there, unseen. A gun or fitting that clips the hull does so above the band |
| STATIC-LANDING | At a death's last frame a part blown off its mount lies on its base (within 5 degrees) and on the ground (within 1 % of the height) |
| STATIC-TRACK-GROUND | In every clip but a death, a tank's tracks and wheels keep their lowest point on the ground, within the largest hull bob on its suspension of vanilla's light tank moves (`rules.move_ground.max_share`, 3.1 % of the height) |
| STATIC-DEATH-RIGID | In every frame of a death, a part blown off its mount (a turret) and everything mounted on it (its gun, its hatches and fittings) move as one rigid body: no two of their points change their distance by more than a thousandth of the main measure. A piece with a channel of its own in the clip, or a fitting left on the hull, hangs in the air apart from the turret |
| STATIC-SHADOW | The colour texture has no cast-shadow region (a band over 5 % of a facet group, much darker than the group's median, sharp-edged, in the group's colour). Only the streams painted with the colour texture count: a tank's track runs, on their own tread texture, are left out (their UVs drawn on the atlas read as a shadow along the run). Tripo's texture, not the route, paints one: make the texture again from a picture lit without cast shadows, or let the route's delighting lift it |
| STATIC-SHADING | At most 1 % of the surface has a corner whose shading normal leans more than 45 degrees (the route's 40 degree crease and a margin) off its own face |
| STATIC-SLAB | A building's or landmark's base slab has a flat top, no corner out of line with its side and no corner that shades tilted (over 8 degrees off flat) |
| STATIC-YARD | A map building's ground yard reaches at most 6 % of the building's size past it (with a fifth more for the reduction's moves) |

`static_sheets.py` renders the close-up review of `review_unit.py` with fixed notes that say what the kind is (a tank has tracks, not feet), a 1024 px close-up of the rest pose from the front and the back quarter (a ship's mast and a plane's gun stubs are small on the six-view sheets), and a game-size sheet: the model and the vanilla model nearest the kind's median size, side by side, at the pixels a metre a vanilla soldier gets on the map. A ship's water is drawn see-through, so its hull shows as it sinks. Give `REVIEW_REQUEST.md` and the sheets to a fresh reviewer as for a unit.

## Other model kinds

The notes below come from models made by hand before the [static route](#static-and-rigid-part-models). They still hold for a model made outside it.

**Buildings and landmarks (not proven yet on a detailed subject).**

- A tall and thin landmark (height over twice the larger side of its upper half) is sized on its height, but its base is bounded too. Vanilla's four (Big Ben, the Eiffel Tower, Hakko Ichiu, the Statue of Liberty) stand on bases of 0.17, 0.43, 0.88 and 0.82 times their height, so the contract's `size.tall_base.max_share` is 0.8757. A model whose largest horizontal side is wider than that share of the height it would get is sized on the base (largest side divided by 0.8757), so a wide paved plaza does not read as a larger monument than vanilla's. STATIC-SIZE measures the same way. An obelisk with a plaza 1.12 times its height wide came out as tall as the Statue of Liberty and 1.40 times as wide at its base. It is now 0.80 times as tall and 1.09 times as wide.
- A building can be textured with no image: one joined mesh, procedural Blender materials, smart UV project and packed islands, then Cycles bakes colour, ambient occlusion, a tangent normal map and the specular maps, packed for the PDX shader. A whole castle baked in about 40 seconds.
- Blender's Brick Texture node samples only two axes, so walls facing one way come out as vertical stripes. Pick the projection per face by its dominant normal axis (triplanar).
- `io_pdx_mesh` refuses faces with more than four corners ("Tangent space can only be computed for tris/quads"). Triangulate before export.
- Every landmark needs a second `collision` shape with the material shader `Collision`. Vanilla landmarks have one 8-vertex box.
- If your mod's state history or `map/buildings.txt` is generated by a tool, do not edit it by hand. Add a new spawn point, keep automatic nudging on, and place the building with an `on_startup` effect that runs `set_building_level` in state scope with the province given. Keep the map rows in a note for the generator.

**Ships (proven on one model).**

- Rebuild vanilla's ship contract exactly, measured from vanilla's destroyer, support ship, freighter, light cruiser and battleship: bow toward -Y, waterline at z = 0, a `root` bone (0,0,0 to 0,0.05,0) with a child `ship` bone (0,0,0.363 to 0,0.25,0.363), every vertex weighted 1.0 to `ship`, mesh data named `<object>Shape` (the `meshsettings` name), material `PDXmat_<object>Shape` with `PdxMeshAdvanced`, the locators `front_moving_water_effect`, `back_moving_water_effect` and `miss_effect_*` as plain empties, `hit_effect_locator1` and `hit_effect_locator2` parented to `ship`, and a `pCube1` collision box. Export with the skeleton option on. That round-trips cleanly through `io_pdx_mesh`.
- Give UV space by part, not evenly. Scale each island to a density by material (hull sides 2.0, sail 1.6, deck 1.0, oars 0.7, underwater hull 0.5), but floor every island at about 0.7 of the reference: islands under a pixel bake black.
- Bake ambient occlusion with 128 samples. 16 leaves speckles along thin painted bands.
- A sail can be a thin closed loft with its emblem drawn in the material from the object's coordinates. It bakes crisp at 1024 with no hand UV and shows from both sides without relying on culling.

**Textures for any 3D model.** Use DXT5 with a full mip chain. The 2D image tool (`convert_to_dds.py`) writes uncompressed BGRA with one mip level, which suits interface images but not models. Put the `.dds` files in the model's folder and run `check_unit_files.py <folder> --repair-textures`, which rewrites them as DXT5 with mips and clears a normal map's blue channel.


## Known limits

- **Armed units.** The separate-weapon route passed review on the orc (see [Armed units](#armed-units)). An attack made for an empty hand does not read as a weapon swing, so each armed unit needs an attack made for its weapon. No weaker model has run the route from the card alone yet.
- **Quadruped mounts.** The Tripo quadruped route has not passed review yet (see [Tripo quadrupeds and mounts](#tripo-quadrupeds-and-mounts)). Its distance weights stretch the skin at the leg joints in the rear and the fall.
- **Wings, tails and extra limbs.** Tripo's humanoid rig has no wing bones. [Secondary chains](#secondary-chains-cloaks-and-wings) give wings their own bones and motion, but the membrane can still cross in front of the body in some clips. Tails and extra limbs have no step yet.
- **Cloaks and armour.** [Secondary chains](#secondary-chains-cloaks-and-wings) stop a long cloak stretching, but some of it can still pass into the body in hard clips. Armour plates whose weight Tripo split between bones still bend a little.
- **Two-handed polearms.** A polearm held rigidly in one hand fails two-handed library swings: it crosses the head and torso and the free hand flails. There is no two-handed grip step yet. Pick one-handed clips, or fit a shorter shaft gripped near its centre.
- **A weapon sinking in a death clip.** No free repair lifts a held prop out of the ground yet. Run `fit_prop.py` again (a higher `--ground-weight`, such as 40 instead of 10, cleared it on one unit), or turn the wrist live in Blender.
- **Torso twist.** Library clips often fail review where the torso twists hard, in attacks and deaths. Expect to look for another preset, and sometimes a small live fix.
- **Static models at vanilla's budget.** A Tripo model of 20,000 triangles collapsed to a tank's 3,948 crumples its hull and running gear and thins the barrel to a blade (STATIC-SHAPE fails). Tripo's own low-poly at the budget is no better: spiky shards and a flat blade for a barrel. Make static models with Tripo's Smart Mesh at about the kind's budget (`triangle_budget` in `config/static_kinds.json`): clean hard-surface pieces, a round barrel, separate wheels and tracks.
- **Tank tracks** scroll only on a tank modelled in pieces (Smart Mesh), where the route finds its track runs. A tank in one piece keeps its tracks in the body's atlas, and its move reads as a bob. The tread is drawn by the kit (plates, a grouser and a gap a link), not taken from Tripo's texture, so close up it shows vanilla-style links in the run's own colour rather than the painted detail.
- **A tank taller than vanilla's in proportion** (a Smart Mesh light tank 0.60 times as tall as it is long, against vanilla's 0.41): the size rule keeps its height within 5 % of vanilla's reference, so its footprint comes out smaller than vanilla's (21 % on that tank). Its open bogie brackets and the small fins on its deck are in the source mesh, not loose flakes or needles, so the cleanup keeps them. A new Tripo source with vanilla's proportions and closed brackets fixes both.
- **Open wall shells and facade notches** (a Smart Mesh factory): walls and window bays modelled as single open surfaces show as hollow recesses, and an open loop larger than 32 edges stays a notch. A new Tripo source fixes it (another Smart Mesh variant, or a closed-surface setting). A kit feature that closes large open loops would risk filling openings that belong to the design.
- **Creases, a notched capstone and thin wings** (a Smart Mesh obelisk and its eagle): a folded face, a rough cap and paper-thin wings are in the source mesh. A new Tripo source fixes them. No kit rule straightens a folded face without guessing the intended shape.
- **A higher Smart Mesh polygon count is not always better** (12,000 asked against about 6,000). The monument's figures got sharper, but its plaza came as loose wedge-shaped slabs with the ground showing between them. The factory's roofs and walls came out wavy, and 3 of its edges stay turned against their neighbours whichever way they face, so UNIT-WINDING fails. Route both counts and keep the one the review finds cleaner.
- **UNIT-PROP-CLIP** reads a point behind an open, one-sided sheet such as a cape as inside the body. Look at the named frames before changing a clip.
- **Review rounds** converge only with fixed notes and scoped rounds. Without them each fresh reviewer raises new minor items.
- **Small looks at game size.** At the map camera a unit is a few dozen pixels tall. A pale face or a torn hem is lost there. Judge fidelity on the close sheets and readability on the game-size sheet.

## When something fails

Ask where a unit stands at any time. This reads files only, costs nothing, and prints the last steps and the exact next command:

```
py -3.13 -B .tools/3d_pipeline/run_pilot.py --jobs-config <your 3d_jobs.json> --status <unit>
```

| Problem | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Tripo's page looks signed out, or the sign-in code page vanishes | Tripo's service may be down for a few minutes. Wait, then load the page again. Nothing is lost: finished models stay in your Assets |
| A paid Tripo button was pressed and nothing seems to happen | Do not press it again. Look at the Assets panel and the credit balance first. A new tile or a lower balance means the task started |
| Tripo's Export button does nothing (no progress, no file) | It happened after many exports in one session. Do not keep pressing it. Look for a blocked-download icon in Chrome's address bar and for Tripo's export limit on your plan, then try again later. The model and its applied clips stay in your Assets |
| A clip in the export has a different name from its tile | Tripo renames on export: the tile `defeat` exports as `defeat_02` and `defeat_02` as `defeat_03`. A text-to-motion clip is named after its instruction. Write the exported name, or its first words, into `tripo.roles` |
| TRIPO-HANDS-HIGH fails | The attack holds the hands above the head. The arm-level default fixes it for an unarmed unit. If it is switched off, switch it on again, or pick another attack |
| TRIPO-NECK-SUNK fails | The neck lift default fixes it. If it is switched off, switch it on again. If it still fails, the lift record in `runtime/files.json` says which joints it tried and how far each got. Pick another clip |
| TRIPO-SHOULDER-SWELL fails | The shoulder ease default fixes most of it. Its record in `runtime/files.json` says which group it turned, how far, and why a group was rejected. What it leaves is usually an arm held over the head whose weights reach the back (the ogre's chop). A cloak over the back that swells is the cloak's business: set `cape: true` and a `cloak` chain in the job |
| TRIPO-SHOULDER-TEAR fails | The upper arm weight rule runs on its own when the clips fail. Its record says whether the trade accepted it and why not. If it was refused, pick a clip whose arm rises less with `tripo/pose_check.py` |
| TRIPO-DEATH-SPLAY fails | The knee swing and the leg closing run by default and their record (`splay` under `runtime_ground` in `runtime/files.json`, the closing under `closing`) says how far they got and why they stopped. The finding names the body's limit and its rest pose's split. It stops where swinging further would deform a frame more than it was, so a death clip that kneels with the thighs far apart can still fail. Try a death clip that falls without a wide kneel |
| TRIPO-ATTACK-NO-STRIKE fails | The attack holds a pose. Pick another attack by the rule in [Pick the clips by measurement](#pick-the-clips-by-measurement) with `pose_check.py --attack`. If none strikes, a text-to-motion clip costs credits: ask first |
| TRIPO-DEATH-LEG-KICK fails | The kick settle runs by default and its record (`kick` under `runtime_ground` in `runtime/files.json`) gives the span it laid the leg across, or why it took none. Try another death clip with `death_pick.py --settled` |
| TRIPO-NOT-DOWN or TRIPO-LIMB-IN-TORSO fails | Pick another death clip by the rule in [Pick the clips by measurement](#pick-the-clips-by-measurement). `defeat_03` passed on a humanoid |
| TRIPO-DEATH-WING-TENT fails | A winged quadruped's dead wings stand over the lying body on the game camera's screen. The check's numbers give the worst frame (`wing_tent_frame`). Look at that frame from the map camera, then try the death's lying wing constants offline (`LYING_UPPER`, `LYING_LOWER`, `DEAD_UPPER`, `DEAD_LOWER` in `quadruped_wings.py`) with `tripo/pose_preview.py --plan` and measure each candidate with `tripo/tripo_checks.py` before spending a run. Round 10 of [Tripo winged quadrupeds](#tripo-winged-quadrupeds) lists what already failed |
| The weapon bends or shears in an animation | The weapon was modelled into the hand and rigged with the body. Redraw the picture with empty hands and make the weapon its own model (see [Armed units](#armed-units)) |
| Tripo's rig prompt sends you to a T-pose picture | Tripo prefers a T-pose or A-pose. The rig still works on a relaxed pose with arms away from the body. Use the T Pose picture template only when the rig fails |
| The rigged export is named `download.glb` | Tripo's server names it. Rename it to the name on the card when you move it into the inbox |
| The intake says it finds no export | The file name must be `<asset>__rigged.glb`, `<asset>__segmented.glb` or `<asset>__model.glb`, in the inbox folder you passed |
| Meshy answers 402 (old Meshy jobs) | Your account is on the free plan. Upgrade or join the contributor program. Nothing was charged |
| "Unreconciled submission" | A paid step may have reached Meshy without an answer. Look the task up in Meshy's task list and record it as the message says. Never start the step again by hand |
| A paid step fails | Run the same command again. Steps that succeeded are reused and never paid twice |
| The body or hole check fails on the model | Read the verdict. Change `unit_type`, drop floating pieces, or ask for a new reference image and one more model |
| "No library clip still matches its key" | Meshy renamed a library clip. Pick another clip for that role in `unit_types.json` |
| "This animation is no longer available" | Meshy retired a clip it still lists. The pipeline moves to the role's next clip by itself and releases the refused call's credits. If no clip is left, add one for that role in `unit_types.json` |
| The quadruped rig cannot find four legs | The legs touch each other or the ground is uneven. Your agent can pass measured landmarks instead |
| A hanging prop (a maul on the belt, a quiver) smears when the unit walks | Meshy weighted a loose part to the nearest limbs. Add `loose_part_edits` to the job: each rule's box, in fractions of the unit's height (x and y from the body's centre, z from the ground), selects the loose parts inside it, which are dropped (`"action": "drop"`) or given to one bone (`"action": "bind", "bone": "Hips"`). Put the reason in `loose_part_edits_reason`. Rerun the job. It costs nothing |
| A check fails after export | Read `runtime/files.json` in the job folder. It names the file and the rule |
| UNIT-ANIM-FPS fails on a clip made by hand | Export it again with `export_clip`, which writes 30 frames a second and keeps the clip's length. A unit whose entity events (sounds, flashes) are timed in another rate's frames keeps that rate: `export_clip(action, path, fps=24)`, and check it with `--fps 24` |
| UNIT-NORMALS fails | No free repair: the winding repair keeps imported normals. In Blender, flip those corner normals, or recalculate them on a welded copy and copy the result back |
| UNIT-STREAM-VERTICES fails after a Blender edit | Run `clear_sharp_faces` on the mesh and export again |
| Holes remain after `repair_unit.py` | Rebuild the faces in Blender, or make a new model |
